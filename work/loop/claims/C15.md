# C15 — mutual pairs: exact shape, and the parity constraint on their line

**Statement (shape; proven).** Suppose P's axis side at X is killed (C10). Then:
- there is a triple point Q, consecutive with P on a line m ∉ {a_P}, such that Q has two
  blocks and is type X with axis a_Q = C (the cap line of P's block at X);
- X = a_P ∩ a_Q is the far end of a block middle at both P and Q;
- the triangle PQX (lines m, a_P, a_Q) is a face, and it is a cap triangle of both points.

*Proof.*
- C10 gives z(b,C,N), where b is a side-ray line of P. So Q = b∩C∩N is the first vertex of
  that side ray, which makes P and Q consecutive on m = b.
- [Q,X] is doubly used with cap line a_P. So [Q,X] is a block middle of Q and a_Q ⊇ [Q,X]
  is C.
- If Q has two blocks, they are disjoint (type X) whenever β = 0, since a bent pair has a
  bridge. ∎

**Statement (parity on the mutual line; proven, from C11).** Assume β = 0. Every type-X point
R on m with a_R ≠ m has single, opposite entries on m: the cap triangles lie in vertically
opposite sectors, and the non-cap sectors are empty by Lemma B. Hence, if
- m is not a cap line,
- no triple point on m has m as its axis, and
- the number j(m) of triple points on m is even,

then **m touches an unused segment**.

**Corollary (k = 6, β = 0; proven with C14).** Z = 0, so every mutual-pair line m is one of:
- (α) a cap line of a block of a point not on m;
- (β′) the axis of some triple point on it;
- (γ) a line carrying an odd number (≥ 3) of triple points.

In particular the "collinear zigzag" (all 6 points on one line m, consecutive mutual pairs,
private axes) is impossible: m has j = 6, it caps nothing (all points are on it), and it is
nobody's axis.

**Kills.** The collinear zigzag for k = 6. For k = 6 with β = 0, the space shrinks to mutual
lines satisfying (α), (β′) or (γ).

**SAT form.** Mutual pair (P = {m,b,…}, Q = {m,…}, axes a, a′):
  z[P] ∧ z[Q] ∧ tri(m,a,a′) ∧ [a∩a′ is a block far end at both]
  ⇔ tri(a,x,a′) ∧ tri(a,y,a′) and tri(a′,x′,a) ∧ tri(a′,y′,a),
where {x,y} and {x′,y′} are the other lines at P and Q. The parity consequence uses C11's
XOR constraint, with ok and rep automatically true when β = 0 and the point is type X.
Cheapest useful encoding: for each line m carrying an even number of z-triples, none of
which has m as its axis (no tri(m,·,C) ∧ tri(m,·,C) block pattern at those points), and m in
no cap role: OR_{M,d} U(m,M,d).

**Empirical.**
- Every mutual pair found in the gallery (n = 18–22, t = 5–9) is consecutive on a shared
  line. The shape statement was checked in all cases.
- The n = 18, t = 8 examples are collinear zigzags with j = 8, yet Z = 0.
- Checked on 18-18kh1heyvi3hs: m's t-sequence is `.-P+P-P+P-P+P-P+P`, i.e. 8 consecutive
  triple points with alternating entries. The last one is the 1-block point, and it is the
  END vertex of m, so C11's hypothesis (ii) fails there (the next entry is the ray).
- So the hypothesis "no triple point is an end vertex of m", which is automatic for
  type-X points, is exactly what these examples use.
