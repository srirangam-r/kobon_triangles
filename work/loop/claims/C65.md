# C65 — converging caps: an all-multiple face has at most one type-X vertex (so 2-F faces do not exist)

**Lemma (proven, lines or pseudolines, any k).**
- *Setting.* PQR is an all-multiple triangular face.
- *Claim.* P and R cannot both have blocks covering their rays toward Q. A block of P *covers* P→Q if its
  middle is the neighbour of P→Q other than P→R.

*Proof.*
1. Circular order at R: R→P, R→Q, γ, R→−P, R→−Q, γ′, where γ and γ′ lie on R's third line b_R.
   - The block of R covering R→Q has middle γ and covers R→Q, γ, R→−P.
   - Its cap triangle in the sector (γ, R→Q) has vertex Q. At Q it lies in the sector next to Q→R on the
     side away from P, and that sector is bounded by Q's third line b_Q. So the cap is **b_Q**.
   - The second cap triangle, in (R→−P, γ), has its apex first(R→−P) on the same cap (C10).
   - So **b_Q ∩ m_PR = first(R→−P)** lies on m_PR beyond R, on the side away from P.
2. By the same argument at P, **b_Q ∩ m_PR = first(P→−R)** lies on m_PR beyond P, on the side away from R.
3. b_Q and m_PR cross once (b_Q ≠ m_PR, since Q ∉ m_PR). The two rays of m_PR are disjoint. Contradiction. ∎

(In straight-line terms: R's block needs b_Q to cross m_PR beyond R, and P's block needs it to cross beyond P.)

**Corollary.** A type-X face vertex has its axis on its third line (C24 F1, k-independent). So it has blocks
covering both of its face rays. Two type-X vertices P and R therefore both cover toward Q, which is
impossible. Hence **no all-multiple face has two type-X vertices**, and 2-F faces do not exist.
- The 2-F-face analyses of C24 (F3, case x_f = 2), C42 (a), C43, C44, C45 (b) and C48 type A are vacuous.
  So is the C43 exception E1/E2: none of them is needed.
