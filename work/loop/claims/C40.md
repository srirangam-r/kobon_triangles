# C40 — centroid arms need 0/1-block partners (proven); the centroid is a triangle with three concurrent cevians

**Setting.** P is a centroid: blocks at r1, r3, r5, with shared rays r0, r2, r4 whose first
vertices Q0, Q2, Q4 are triple.
- Caps: C1 ∋ Q0, X1, Q2; C3 ∋ Q2, X3, Q4; C5 ∋ Q4, X5, Q0. All are consecutive on the cap.
- Lines of P: ℓ0 = r0∪r3, ℓ1 = r1∪r4, ℓ2 = r2∪r5.

**Claim 1 (proven).** Each Q_i has at most one block, and its middle is the ray of line PQ_i
beyond Q_i.

*Proof for Q2.* Q2's lines are ℓ2, C1 and C3. The sectors at Q2 next to Q2→P are the cap
triangles PX1Q2 and PQ2X3. So Q2's rays in circular order are:
C3→X3, Q2→P, C1→X1, C3′, ℓ2′, C1′.
- **Middle C1→X1.** The cap would be ℓ1, the other line through X1. It would have to meet C3′
  at its first vertex. But ℓ1 ∩ C3 = Q4, since r4 ⊂ ℓ1 has first vertex Q4 ∈ C3, and Q4 lies
  on the X3 side of Q2. Impossible.
- **Middle C3→X3.** Symmetric: the cap is ℓ0, and ℓ0 ∩ C1 = Q0 lies on the X1 side.
- **Middle C1′.** The cap passes through X3, so it is ℓ0, and it must meet C1′. But
  ℓ0 ∩ C1 = Q0 lies on the X1 side. Impossible.
- **Middle C3′.** Symmetric: the cap is ℓ1, and ℓ1 ∩ C3 = Q4.
- **Middle Q2→P.** Its far end is triple, so it cannot be a middle.

Only ℓ2′ remains. ∎ This is verdict C08's argument. It uses only the two blocks next to the
arm, and the fact that the adjacent block's cap meets the next ray's first vertex. So bent
arms and centroid arms are covered by one proof.

**Claim 2 (proven).** Arm capacity (C21 Claim 1) holds for any mix of bent and centroid arms.
A point Q receives at most 2 arms. If it receives 2, they are opposite, and Q has no block.
- Adjacent arms: the first vertex of the neighbouring ray is a simple cap point.
- Alternating arms: they force a block at Q off both arm lines, which contradicts Claim 1.
- Opposite arms: Claim 1 would point Q's block middle at the other arm's point.

**Structure (proven).**
- The six lines ℓ0, ℓ1, ℓ2, C1, C3, C5 form a triangle Q0Q2Q4 with sides C1, C3, C5 and
  three cevians concurrent at P, with feet X1, X3, X5.
- Each Q_i is a vertex of this triangle, and its only possible block middle points outward
  along its cevian.
- By C35, each Q_i needs its arm's two sectors, which are the small inner triangles.
- By C39, Q_i is O1a if its side rays, the sides of the triangle beyond Q_i, have simple
  first vertices.
