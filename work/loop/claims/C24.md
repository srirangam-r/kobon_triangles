# C24 — k = 4: all-multiple faces with a type-X vertex close

**Setting.** k = 4, and PQR is an all-multiple triangular face (P, Q, R pairwise consecutive),
S is the fourth point, and at least one face vertex is type X. Master count, as in C23:
Φ ≥ σ − 2β + Σm. A 94 needs Φ ≤ −6.

**Face-vertex facts (proven).** Label rays at P as r0 = →Q, r1 = →R (face in (r0,r1)),
r3 = opp r0, r4 = opp r1, and r2, r5 on P's third line b_P.

- **(F1) An X face vertex P.**
  - Its blocks are exactly {r2, r5}: its axis is b_P (C4 note (b), k = 3 analysis).
  - Block r2 covers r1, so its cap passes through R. The outer triangle on [P,R] lies in
    R's sector next to R→P on the outer side, bounded by R's third line. So the cap is b_R.
    Likewise block r5 is capped by b_Q.
  - So x_P = 2 and m_P ≥ −1, and both face sides at P are doubly used.
  - P has no other bridge: a second all-multiple face at P would need triple first
    vertices on both r3 and r4, but only S is left.
- **(F2) Three X face vertices are impossible.** Six lines would carry 10 triangular faces
  but have ≤ 7 bounded faces (C4 note (b); this holds even if S is a crossing of the b's).
- **(F3) A non-X face vertex R when both other face vertices are X.**
  - The outer triangles make b_R's first vertices X_PR = b_P∩b_R and X_QR = b_Q∩b_R.
  - A block of R centred on an away ray (q3 or q4) would have its cap through X_PR or
    X_QR. That cap would be b_P or b_Q, which meets the needed ray only at P or Q, on the
    wrong side. Impossible.
  - So R's block, if any, is on b_R and is capped by b_Q or b_P: x_R = 1 and m_R ≥ 1.
    Otherwise R has no block and m_R = 3.
  - R is not bent: the middles next to its away rays are impossible.

**Case x_f = 2** (two X face vertices P, Q; R as in F3).
- All three face sides are doubly used. P and Q have no bridge to S (F1).
- *No bridge R–S:* β = 3 and σ ≥ 3.
  - S isolated: Φ ≥ 3 − 6 − 2 + 1 − 1 = −5.
  - S not isolated: σ ≥ 4 and Φ ≥ −5.
  - This is the −6 sub-case of C23, now closed by m_R ≥ 1.
- *Bridge R–S:* S lies on m_PR or m_QR beyond R, so σ ≥ 4 and β = 4, giving
  Φ ≥ −6 + m_R + m_S.
  - S cannot be X: its bridge would need a face SRT, but S, R, P are collinear, and at R
    the rays toward S and toward Q are not adjacent.
  - If S is bent, its partner is R. R's block middle would then point at P, so R has no
    block (m_R = 3) and Φ ≥ −4.
  - If S has one block: Φ ≥ −5.
  - If S has no block: Φ ≥ −3.

**Case x_f = 1** (P is X; Q and R are not). Label Q's rays as ρ_R, ρ_P, u (b_Q toward
X_PQ), opp ρ_R, opp ρ_P, u′.
- *Q's possible blocks:*
  - u: capped by b_P, a mutual pair;
  - opp ρ_P: possible;
  - u′: capped by an R-line;
  - opp ρ_R: impossible (the cap would be b_P, which meets m_PQ only at P).
  - The only 2-block option is bent toward S = first vertex of opp ρ_R, with blocks {u, opp ρ_P}.
- *S without bridges:* β ≤ 3, and Φ ≥ 3 − 6 − 1 + 0 + 0 + m_S ≥ −5. When S is X and not
  isolated, σ ≥ 4.
- *S with bridges:* each S-bridge adds a distinct +1 to σ, so σ ≥ 3 + β_S.
  - **Neither Q nor R bent:**
    - Bridges S–Q, S–R and [Q,R] all doubly used: a second face SQR with S = b_Q∩b_R.
      - Then Q's and R's single blocks have triple caps: u is capped by b_P, and opp ρ_P
        by an S-line through the first vertex of u′ = S. So m_Q, m_R ≥ 1.
      - With σ = 5 and β = 5: Φ ≥ −5 − 1 + 2 + m_S ≥ −5. If S is X, then x_S = 2 and m_S ≥ −1.
    - Otherwise Φ ≥ −4 − β_S + m_Q + m_R + m_S. The remaining dangerous options are all
      fine:
      - S with one block and β_S ≤ 1: fine;
      - S bent toward Q: Q's block lies on u or u′ (verdict C08: the middle is on line SQ
        beyond Q, which rules out opp ρ_P), so m_Q ≥ 1 and Φ ≥ −5;
      - S bent with [Q,R] singly used: Φ ≥ −5;
      - S of type X with [Q,R] singly used: impossible, since the face SQR would make
        [Q,R] doubly used.
  - **Q bent toward S:**
    - C19 forbids the bridge triangle PQR, so [Q,R] is singly used. Also b_P passes through S.
    - So σ ≥ 5 (m_QR carries R, Q, S; b_P carries P, S) and β = 3.
    - Φ ≥ 5 − 6 − 2 + m_R + m_S ≥ −4.
    - If both Q and R are bent toward S, C21 forces S to have no block: Φ ≥ −3.

**Conclusion (proven, modulo refereed ingredients).** Every all-multiple face configuration
with an X vertex at k = 4 has Φ ≥ −5. **No 94.**

**SAT form.**
- For z-triples P, Q, R pairwise consecutive (face) with P of X type (blocks on b_P): the
  caps of P are the third lines of Q and R.
- If Q is also X: R's block is on b_R with a triple cap, or R has no block.
- Cheap consequence: an all-multiple face has at most 2 X vertices, and if it has 2, the
  third vertex has no block off its third line.

**Empirical.** The referee's run found 146 axis bridges, all in faces with the F1 cap
structure (C07 verdict). Not separately tested.
