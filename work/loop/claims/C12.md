# C12 — the k = 4 "two mutual pairs" pattern is impossible (closes C8's open pattern)

**Pattern (from general.md §6).**
- 4 triple points: P1, P2 consecutive on m and P3, P4 consecutive on m′; no other sharing
  (σ = 2).
- All four have two disjoint blocks with private axes b_i.
- Mutual pairs: (b1,b2) at b1∩b2 and (b3,b4) at b3∩b4. So P1's block toward P2 is capped by
  b2, and P2's block is capped by b1; likewise for P3, P4.
- D = 8, and a 94 needs Z ≤ 2.

**Claim.** No 94 has this pattern.

**Proof.**
- *If x ≥ 5 (some other cap line is a triple line):* at most 3 of the 8 non-triple lines
  are caps, so clean ≥ 5 and 2Z ≥ 5. Then Z ≥ 3 > 2.
- *If x = 4:* m and m′ are cap lines of nothing. P1 and P2 each have two disjoint blocks,
  their axes b1, b2 differ from m, and no all-multiple triangular face exists. For the last
  point: P1's lines other than m carry no other triple point, so Lemma B's exception cannot
  occur.
- So by C11 the line m (j = 2, q = 0) touches an unused segment at a simple crossing, and so
  does m′.
- Neither is clean. Therefore 2Z ≥ clean + 2 = (8 − 4) + 2 = 6, so Z ≥ 3 > 2. ∎

**Kills.** C8's residual pattern entirely. The k = 4 open space now excludes it; C8 should
be marked closed by C12, pending referee.

**SAT form.** The general C11 clause suffices. As a direct, cheaper form, for the k = 4 run:
- for every line L with exactly two z-triples on it, both having cap roles on their two
  other lines (≥ 4 tri with those lines);
- if L is in no ("cap",L,t), then OR_{M,d} U(L,M,d).

**Empirical.** No sample of this exact pattern exists in the data. C11 was tested as above.
