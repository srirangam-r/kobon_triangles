# A02. Block-cap charging: a multiple first vertex on a block puts its cap in x

**Family.** Any n, any multiplicities (used here for n = 18, t >= 7, no 4-fold point).
x is as in `work/t3/general.md`: the number of pairs (P, C) where C is a cap line of a block
of P and C passes through a multiple point other than P.

## Statement

Let P be a multiple point with a block of rays (r-1, r, r+1), middle ray r and cap line C,
and let V_{r-1}, V_r, V_{r+1} be the first vertices of those three rays.

1. All three of V_{r-1}, V_r, V_{r+1} lie on C, and V_r is simple.
2. **If V_{r-1} or V_{r+1} is a multiple point, then (P, C) is counted in x.**
3. In particular, if the first segment of an outer ray r-1 or r+1 is doubly used, then it is a
   bridge (its far end is multiple by L1 applied at the far end: its far end cannot be the
   simple cap point V_r) and (P, C) is in x.
4. **Ray bookkeeping at a triple point** (6 rays, block middles pairwise non-adjacent):
   - *axis point* (middles opposite): every non-middle ray is an outer ray of a block, so
     **every bridge at an axis point charges a block of that point**, and an axis point with
     a bridge has a cap line through another triple point;
   - *bent point* (middles at distance 2): the shared outer ray and the two other outer rays
     charge; only the ray opposite the shared ray ("back ray") does not;
   - *1-block point*: only the two outer rays charge; rays at distance 2 and 3 from the
     middle do not;
   - *0-block point*: no ray charges;
   - *centroid* (middles 0, 2, 4): the three bridges 1, 3, 5 are outer rays of two blocks
     each, so all three caps carry a triple point and x(P) = 3.
5. **Payment reading.** Each bridge costs 2 in Phi = sigma + x - 2 beta + sum_P h_P + Cred.
   A bridge charges at most one block per endpoint, and a block is charged by at most its two
   outer rays, so x >= (number of charged bridge ends)/2. With A01 (sigma >= beta) a bridge
   whose two ends both charge is fully paid: 1 from sigma, 1/2 + 1/2 from x. The only
   bridges that are not fully paid are those with an end at a bent back ray, at a 1-block
   point on a ray at distance 2 or 3 from the middle, or at a 0-block point.

## Proof

1. By definition of a block, C is the first line crossing the three consecutive rays, so the
   first crossings of those rays are the points of C; V_r is simple because the middle
   segment [P, V_r] is doubly used with a simple far end (L1: otherwise it is a bridge and
   not a block middle).
2. Immediate from 1: C then passes through the multiple point V_{r±1} != P.
3. A doubly used first segment is a block middle or a bridge (L1). Its far end V_{r±1} lies
   on C. If V_{r±1} were simple the segment would be a second block middle at P with middle
   ray r±1, which is impossible: block middles are non-adjacent rays (verdict C07). So
   V_{r±1} is multiple and 2 applies.
4. Pure ray arithmetic on the 6-cycle of rays: with middles {0, 3} the non-middle rays
   1, 2, 4, 5 are each adjacent to a middle; with middles {0, 2} the ray 4 is adjacent to
   3 and 5 only; with a single middle 0 the rays 2, 3, 4 are not adjacent to it; with middles
   {0, 2, 4} every odd ray is adjacent to two middles.
5. Counting only. The "charged block" of a bridge end is the block whose outer ray carries
   it; if both adjacent rays are middles (centroid) pick either, which only lowers the count.

## SAT form

For each block literal `blk(P, r, C)` and each outer ray r' in {r-1, r+1}: 
`blk(P, r, C) and dbl(P, r') -> xpair(P, C)`, and `xpair(P, C)` is the literal already used
for "cap line through a multiple point" in the k = 5 / k = 6 builders. For axis points the
stronger form is a unit implication: `axis(P) and dbl(P, r') -> xpair(P, C(r'))` for every
non-middle ray r'. The payment inequality of 5 can be added as the cardinality constraint
`2 beta <= sigma + 2 x + (number of bridge ends at bent back rays, 1-block far rays and
0-block points)`, all of whose terms are already literals in the C34 encoding.

## Test

`python3 work/t3/a_struct.py --selftest 200`

The checker verifies, for every bridge end adjacent to a block middle, that the far end is a
multiple point lying exactly on that block's cap line, and that no bridge at an axis point
fails to be adjacent to a block middle. Result: 259 positive instances over 200 random exact
arrangements plus the C07 bent fixture (which has 4 bridges at one bent point, 4 charged
instances there), **no failures**. 235 bridge ends were uncharged, all of them at 1-block or
0-block points or at a bent back ray, as item 4 predicts.
