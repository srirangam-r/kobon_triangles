# C26 — rigidity of singleton ends in C16 (A)/(B): axes, 2nd and 3rd vertices, matching

**Setting.** k = 6, β = 0, Z = 0 (C14, C16), with the singleton set S fixed by the case.
A singleton is a line end (ℓ, e) that is not a wedge (C17). By the C16 proof it is a
cap-point end.

**Claim (proven).** For every singleton (ℓ, e), counting vertices of ℓ from end e:
- (a) X_1 = ℓ∩C is simple, and X_2 is a triple point P = ℓ∩b∩c with axis ℓ. Also
  tri(ℓ,C,b) and tri(ℓ,C,c): P's block at X_1, capped by C.
- (b) X_3 = ℓ∩D is simple, with tri(ℓ,b,D) and tri(ℓ,c,D): P's other block, capped by D.
  This side is killed (mutual), so:
  - D is the axis of a point Q lying on b or on c, consecutive with P there;
  - tri(ℓ,D,m), where m ∈ {b,c} is the line PQ: the face PQX_3;
  - Q's block at X_3 is capped by ℓ, i.e. tri(D,x,ℓ) and tri(D,y,ℓ), where x, y are
    Q's other two lines.
- (c) In (A), where every point is 2nd on its axis:
  - the six singletons are perfectly matched, (ℓ,e) ↔ (ℓ′,e′), by mutual pairs;
  - ℓ∩ℓ′ is the **3rd vertex from e on ℓ and the 3rd vertex from e′ on ℓ′**;
  - if a line carries two singletons, it is the axis of two points, one 2nd from each end.
- (d) In (B):
  - the four singleton points have mutual degree 1 and the two others degree 2;
  - the mutual graph (4 edges on 6 points) is either two paths P–A–P′ and Q–B–Q′, or a
    path P–A–B–P′ plus an edge Q–Q′;
  - the endpoints of every path are singleton points, and (c) holds for each
    singleton–singleton edge;
  - every consecutive pair of triple points on a shared line is a mutual pair (C16 (B)).

**Proof.**
- (a): C16's line-end analysis. A singleton end has t_1 = "both", so l_1 is a block middle,
  X_2 = P, and ℓ = a_P.
- (b): P is killed (C14). Its X_3 side is bounded (a line has ≥ 12 vertices), so it is a
  mutual side (C10, C15; under C14, Q is type X).
- (c), (d): counting from C16, plus the fact that a mutual pair joins a side of P to a side
  of Q at the same point X = a_P∩a_Q.
  - In (A) both sides involved are the non-singleton sides, i.e. X_3 on both lines. ∎

**SAT form (given S).** For each singleton (ℓ, e), write "from e" for before or after on ℓ,
according to e.
- **2nd vertex triple:** ∃C first_e(ℓ,C) ∧ ∃b<c: z[ℓbc] ∧ A_e(ℓ,C,b), plus tri(ℓ,C,b) ∧
  tri(ℓ,C,c). Equivalently, exactly 3 lines cross ℓ strictly before the 3rd vertex, and two
  of them are concurrent with ℓ.
- **(A) matching:** for each singleton (ℓ,e) there is a singleton (ℓ′,e′) with ℓ′ ≠ ℓ such
  that:
  - #{M : M crosses ℓ strictly before ℓ′ from e} = 3;
  - #{M : M crosses ℓ′ strictly before ℓ from e′} = 3.
  - Cardinality via the before literals; this has 5 choices per singleton, so 15 perfect
    matchings.
- **(A) axes:** the singleton lines are exactly the axis lines. A line carrying two
  singletons has both of its end-2nd vertices triple.
- **Blocks and faces:**
  - tri(ℓ,b,ℓ′) ∧ tri(ℓ,c,ℓ′) for the P-block at X_3;
  - tri(ℓ,m,ℓ′) for the face.

**Empirical.** Not testable (these are 94 configurations).
