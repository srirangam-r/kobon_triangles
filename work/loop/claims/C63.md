# C63 — fan lemma: a point with four consecutive triple-ended rays, the outer two bridges, has no block unless a fifth ray is a bridge

**Lemma (proven, lines or pseudolines, any k).**
- *Setting.* Q is a triple point with rays ρ0..ρ5 in circular order.
  - ρ0, ρ1, ρ2, ρ3 have triple first vertices.
  - ρ0 and ρ3 are doubly used, i.e. bridges.
- *Claim.* Every block of Q has its middle at ρ4 or ρ5. A block at ρ4 makes ρ5 a bridge, and a block at ρ5
  makes ρ4 a bridge.

*Proof.*
- A block middle needs a simple first vertex, so it can only be ρ4 or ρ5.
- Suppose the block is at ρ4. Then the sectors (ρ3,ρ4) and (ρ4,ρ5) are triangles. The sector (ρ5,ρ0) is a
  triangle too, because ρ0 is doubly used.
- So ρ5 borders two triangles and is doubly used.
- ρ5 is not a block. Adjacent block middles are impossible (L2):
  - if their caps were distinct, they would share the rays ρ4 and ρ5;
  - if their caps were equal, that cap would cross four consecutive rays first.
- Hence ρ5 has a triple far end and is a bridge. The case ρ5 is symmetric. ∎

**Corollary (fan).** Let Q lie in three all-multiple faces that are consecutive around Q, in the sectors
(ρ0,ρ1), (ρ1,ρ2) and (ρ2,ρ3). Let ρ0 and ρ3 be bridges.
- ρ1 and ρ2 each border two faces, so they are bridges, and e(Q) ≥ 4.
- A block at Q would force e(Q) ≥ 5.
- Two bridges from Q to the same point would share two lines, so e(Q) ≤ k − 1. **At k = 5, Q has no block (O0).**
