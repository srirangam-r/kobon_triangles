# C53 — 4-fold points as bent or centroid-arm partners

**(a) The ≤ 1-block conclusion fails for 4-fold partners.**
- *Setting.* Let P be bent toward a 4-fold point Q, or let Q be a centroid arm partner. Q's lines are PQ, C1, C2 (P's caps) and a fourth line L4.
- The sectors next to Q→P are P's cap triangles. So Q's rays in circular order are: Q→P, C1→X1, L4a, C2′, PQ′, C1′, L4b, C2→X3.
- A block at C1→X1 has cap ℓ1, and needs ℓ1 through the first vertex of L4a. Unlike the triple case, where that ray was C2′ and ℓ1 ∩ C2 lay on the wrong side, nothing forbids this.
- So C08/C40/C21 capacity does **not** transfer to 4-fold partners.

**(b) Non-rich 4-fold partner: reduction (proven, using the fixed C51).**
- If t(Q) ≤ 5, move L4 itself (a local pseudoline move, or a rotation about another multiple point on L4, per the C51 fix).
- L4's two gaining sets partition Q's 8 sectors, so one of them has ≤ 2 triangles.
- Q becomes the triple point (PQ, C1, C2), P stays bent toward it, and T′ ≥ T. Then C08/C40 apply.

**(c) Rich 4-fold partner.**
- C52 gives m′_Q ≥ 0.5 however many arms point at Q, so the master-inequality accounting is unaffected.
- Only structural pruning in mixed-multiplicity profile filters loses the capacity rule.

**Conclusion.** C52's gap does not affect the per-point master bounds. A mixed filter must allow bent and centroid arms to point at rich 4-fold points without the capacity rule.

**Fix (verdict C53).** In (b) the correct conclusion is "yields a 94 with one fewer 4-fold point". "P stays bent" holds only when L4's gaining set G = {s1,s3,s4,s6} has ≤ 2 triangles. P's cap triangles s0 and s7 lie in G′.
