# C28 — C16 (B): every mutual path or edge joins singleton ends at cyclic distance exactly 5

**Setting.** C16 (B) (refereed-correct): k = 6, β = 0, Z = 0, u = 4, μ = σ = 4.
- There are 4 singleton points (mutual degree 1, 2nd on their axes) and 2 degree-2 points
  A, B (both axis sides mutual).
- Every consecutive pair of triple points on a shared line is a mutual pair.
- The 4 non-triple lines each cap exactly one block, namely one of the 4 non-mutual blocks.
- C26 (d): the mutual graph is either (i) paths P–A–P″ and Q–B–Q″, or (ii) a path
  P–A–B–P′ plus an edge Q–Q′.

**Preliminary facts (proven).**
- (F1) A line through a singleton point, other than its mutual line, carries no other
  triple point. A consecutive pair on it involving the point would have to be mutual, and
  the point's only partner lies on the mutual line.
  - So c_P (P's third line) and a_P carry only P, and the same holds for a_A.
  - For a degree-2 point A whose partners are not collinear with A, A's lines are a_A,
    m_{PA} and m_{AB}.
- (F2) The non-mutual caps C_P are non-triple and pairwise distinct.
- (F3) Take a degree-2 point A with partners at its cap points X and X′.
  - If the partners are not collinear through A, the two faces (partner, A, cap point)
    lie on the same side of a_A.
  - If they are collinear, the faces lie on opposite sides.
  - *Proof:* A's rays are r0..r5 with middles r0 and r3. The partner rays are r1 or r5,
    and r2 or r4. They lie on the same side exactly when the pair is (r1, r2) or (r5, r4).
    The pairs (r1, r4) and (r2, r5) are on a common line.

**Claim 1 (proven, non-collinear paths).** Take a path P–…–P′ whose degree-2 points are not
collinear with their partners. Then the singleton ends of a_P and a_{P′} are at cyclic
distance **exactly 5**. The 4 ends strictly between them belong to C_P, c_P, c_{P′} and
C_{P′}.
- *The curve.* Let Γ run:
  - along a_P from infinity through X_1 and P to X;
  - along the axes of the degree-2 points between consecutive cap points;
  - along a_{P′} from its cap point through P′ and X′_1 to infinity.
- *Γ is simple, and its vertices are only the listed points* (by F1).
- *The face side R.* By F3 all the mutual faces lie on one side R of Γ. The continuations
  of every axis beyond the turning points lie on the other side.
- *No line lies entirely inside R.* Such a line would have to meet a_P beyond X, and that
  part is outside R.
- *Lines crossing Γ twice.* These are only the mutual lines m. Each one's middle segment
  borders a mutual face in R, so both of its ends lie outside R.
- *Lines crossing Γ once.* These are exactly C_P, c_P, c_{P′} and C_{P′}, which are
  distinct by F1 and F2.
- So R's arc at infinity contains exactly 4 ends. ∎

For a singleton–singleton edge Q–Q′, C27 applies and the count is exactly 4 by F1 and F2,
again distance 5.

**Claim 2 (proven): the collinear sub-cases are impossible.**
- *Shape (i), P, A, P″ on m.*
  - Let Γ′ = a_P's singleton ray ∪ [P, P″] on m ∪ a_{P″}'s singleton ray.
  - The rays a_P→X and a_{P″}→X′ lie on opposite sides of Γ′, because X and X′ lie on
    opposite sides of m.
  - Neither ray meets Γ′ (F1). Every line meets both a_P and a_{P″}.
  - So every line passes through a vertex of Γ′: one of X_1, P, A, P″, X″_1.
  - Only 9 distinct lines do: a_P, C_P, m, c_P, a_A, n_A, a_{P″}, c_{P″}, C_{P″}.
    Since 9 < 18, this is a contradiction.
- *Shape (ii), exactly one of A, B collinear* (say P, A, B on m, B not collinear).
  - Let Γ‴ = a_P's singleton ray ∪ [P, B] on m ∪ [B, P′] on m_{BP′} ∪ a_{P′}'s singleton ray.
  - Using F3 at B, the rays a_P→X and a_{P′}→X_{BP′} lie on opposite sides of Γ‴.
  - Every line then passes through one of X_1, P, A, B, P′, X′_1. That allows at most
    11 < 18 lines. Contradiction.
- *Shape (ii), both collinear.*
  - Then P, A, B, P′ all lie on m, which caps nothing (all caps are axes or the
    non-triple lines).
  - m is nobody's axis, carries j = 4 type-X points (C14), and no triple point is an end
    of m.
  - By C11 (with the C15 fix) m touches an unused segment. That contradicts Z = 0.

**SAT form (given S).**
- Match the 4 singletons in pairs (s, s′) with cyclic distance exactly 5.
- The 4 ends strictly between each pair belong to lines through the 1st or 2nd vertex of
  the two singleton lines, counted from their singleton ends.

**Empirical.** Not applicable (these are 94 configurations).
