# C43 — second-apex credit in a 2-F face; the F-heavy residue is rigid

**Lemma (proven).**
- *Setting.* Let PQR be an all-multiple face in which P and R are type X, and Q is the O1b
  vertex (C42). Suppose Q's block is mutual with P: it points toward X_PQ = b_P ∩ b_Q and is
  capped by b_P.
- *Claim.* Then R's axis side at X_RQ = b_R ∩ b_Q is killed only if
  **V := b_Q ∩ (m_PR beyond R)** is a triple point. Otherwise the segment of b_R beyond X_RQ
  is unused (if bounded) and is touched by b_Q at X_RQ. That is a Cred +1, distinct from all
  other counted credits.
- *Proof.*
  - R's block at X_RQ has cap b_Q, and its cap triangles have apexes Q and V.
  - C10: a kill needs an apex with a block toward X_RQ capped by b_R.
  - Q's single block points toward X_PQ instead. So only V can do it. ∎

**Consequence.** Each 2-F face gives **+1 Cred** unless one of two things happens at its
non-mutual F vertex R:
- **(E1)** the fifth point S sits at V. Then S lies on m_PR (a 3-point line) and on b_Q, so
  σ rises by up to 2 unless those incidences are already counted;
- **(E2)** X_RQ is an end vertex of b_R, i.e. R is 2nd on its axis from that end.

**Applied to the two largest classes.**
- **(F,F,V,O1b,O1b)** (15 graphs): master slack 0 in the example. With the +1 it dies,
  unless E1 (S ∈ {the V point, the other O1b}) or E2 holds at its single 2-F face.
- **(F,F,F,O1b,O1b)** (14 graphs): two 2-F faces with master slack 1. So both faces must
  have E1 or E2.

**Rigid residue spec (for the solver).** Per profile:

  filter);
- plus, for every 2-F face (P,Q,R) with Q mutual with P, the disjunction
  **E1 ∨ E2**, where:
  - **E1:** z[S(m_PR, b_Q, ·)] at the vertex after R on m_PR;
  - **E2:** first(b_R, b_Q) ∨ last(b_R, b_Q), i.e. X_RQ is b_R's end.

**Status.** The lemma is proven; the class kills are conditional on excluding E1 and E2.
