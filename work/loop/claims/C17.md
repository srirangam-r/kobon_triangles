# C17 — mutual ends are slope-adjacent ("wedges at infinity"), and what Z = 0 forces

**Statement 1 (any arrangement without parallels, lines or pseudolines).**
- Setting: lines are labelled 0..n−1 by slope, which is the wiring order. X = L ∩ R is a
  simple point that is an end vertex of both L and R.
- Conclusion: L and R are cyclically adjacent among the 2n line ends at infinity, whose
  circular order is 0,1,…,n−1,0,1,…,n−1. Concretely:
  - X first on both ⇒ |L − R| = 1;
  - X last on both ⇒ |L − R| = 1;
  - X first on one and last on the other ⇒ {L,R} = {0, n−1}.

*Proof.*
- The sector at X between the two rays beyond X contains no vertex on its boundary, so it
  is a face.
- Any other (pseudo)line with an end strictly inside that sector's arc at infinity has its
  other end at the antipodal position. That position lies in the opposite sector.
- So the line would cross the sector's boundary, i.e. one of the two rays or X itself.
  That is impossible.
- Hence the two rays are consecutive at infinity. In the wiring model the left ends are
  0..n−1 from top to bottom and the right ends are n−1..0, which gives the stated
  adjacencies. ∎

**Statement 2 (Z = 0 with no triple point at a line end).** Let L be a line whose first
segment carries exactly one triangle (entry t_1 single). Then:
- L's first vertex X_1 is a mutual end with the other line R_1;
- the corner face between their first segments is a triangle.

*Proof.* R_1's piece on side −t_1 borders only the ray face and a non-triangle, so it is
unused. Z = 0 forces that piece to be a ray. The triangle over l_1 on side t_1 uses R_1's
first segment. ∎

**Kills / use.**
- With Statement 2, in C16's sub-patterns every line end that is not a cap-point end is a
  wedge between slope-neighbours.
- So (A) needs exactly 15 wedges with 6 singleton ends, and (B) needs 16 wedges with 4
  singletons.
- A brute force over singleton sets (`work/t3/wedges.py`) found this does **not** kill
  (A) or (B): 5,412 admissible singleton patterns for (A) and 576 for (B). It is a strong
  pruning constraint, not a kill.

**SAT form (cheap, unconditional).** Let first(L,R) := ∧_{M≠L,R} ¬before(L,M,R) ∧ ¬z-shared.
That is, L∩R is L's first vertex and is simple. Define last(L,R) similarly. Then:
- for all pairs with |L−R| ≥ 2 and {L,R} ≠ {0,n−1}: ¬(first(L,R) ∧ first(R,L)) and
  ¬(last(L,R) ∧ last(R,L)), plus the two mixed combinations;
- for |L−R| = 1: forbid first(L,R) ∧ last(R,L) and last(L,R) ∧ first(R,L);
- for {0,n−1}: forbid first∧first and last∧last.

For k6z, add Statement 2: first(L,R) ∧ "t_1 single" ⇒ first(R,L) ∨ last(R,L).

**Empirical.** 828,232 mutual end points (gallery plus mutations, n = 8–22), 0 violations
(`work/t3/mutual_ends.py`).
