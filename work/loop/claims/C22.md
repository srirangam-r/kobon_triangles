# C22 — at k = 4 a bent point has no back-ray bridge; e(bent) ≤ 3 (kills C21's V5 sub-case)

**Statement.** Let k = 4 and let P be bent toward Q (labels as in C19). Then P has no doubly
used bridge on ray 5 (the back ray), so e(P) ≤ 3 (rays 2, 0, 4).

**Proof.**
- A doubly used first segment on ray 5 needs triangles in sectors (4,5) and (5,0).
- The triangle in (5,0) makes ray 0's first segment doubly used, since ray 0 is already
  used by the cap triangle (0,1). Its far end V0 is then multiple (C07 fix), so [P,V0] is
  a doubly used bridge. The same argument gives a bridge [P,V4].
- So P would have bridges on rays 2, 0, 4 and 5, whose first vertices Q, V0, V4, V5 are
  four distinct triple points (they lie on different rays).
- With k = 4 only three other triple points exist. Contradiction. ∎

**Kills.** The V5 sub-case of C21 Claim 3. Every extra bridge of a bent point at k = 4 goes to
V0 or V4, so C21 Claim 3's proven sub-case now covers the whole 4-cycle
(P1Q1, Q1P2, P2Q2, Q2P1).

**SAT form.** At k = 4: bent(P→Q) ⇒ ¬dblbridge on P's back ray. Equivalently, the first
segment of line PQ beyond P (away from Q) is not a side of two triangles.

**Empirical.** Not separately tested (samples are too few).
