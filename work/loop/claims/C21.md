# C21 — several bent points: at most two per partner, opposite, and then the partner has no block

**Setting.** k = 4 triple points, no 4-fold point. Known (refereed, verdicts/C08.md): if P is
bent toward Q, then Q has 0 or 1 blocks, and Q's block, if any, has its middle on line PQ
beyond Q. The master count is Φ ≥ −β + Σ_P m_P, using σ ≥ β. The values are:
- m = −1 for a bent point;
- m ≥ 0 for a 1-block point;
- m = 3 for a 0-block point;
- m ≥ −2 for a type-X point, and ≥ −1 if the X point is isolated.

A 94 needs Φ ≤ −6.

**Claim 1 (proven).** Let P1 and P2 be bent toward the same Q. Then P1 and P2 lie on one line
through Q, on opposite sides of Q, and Q has no block. Hence at most two points are bent
toward any Q. Label Q's rays s0..s5 and let P_i be the first vertex of ray s_{j_i}.
- *Adjacent rays (j2 = j1 ± 1).* P1's two cap triangles at Q occupy the sectors next to
  s_{j1}. Their sides along s_{j1±1} end at P1's block far ends X1 and X3, which are simple.
  So the first vertex of s_{j1±1} is simple, and it cannot be P2.
- *Alternating rays (j2 = j1 + 2).* The ray s_{j1+1} carries a cap triangle of P1 on one
  side and a cap triangle of P2 on the other. Its first segment is then doubly used with a
  simple far end, so it is a block of Q.
  - The middle of that block, s_{j1+1}, lies on Q's third line, not on QP1 or QP2.
  - This contradicts the refereed fact about where Q's block middle must lie.
- *Opposite rays (j2 = j1 + 3).* This is the only remaining option. Q's block middle would
  have to lie on QP1 beyond Q, i.e. on the ray toward P2, whose first vertex is triple. So
  Q has no block.
- Three bent points toward Q would need three rays pairwise at distance 3. That is
  impossible. ∎

**Claim 2 (proven): the sub-case "two bent points toward one Q" closes.**
- The configuration: P1, Q, P2 lie on m with Q between them. [P1,Q] and [Q,P2] are
  bridges, and Q is a 0-block point (m_Q = 3). Let S be the fourth point, and β_S the
  number of bridges at S.
- Φ ≥ −(2 + β_S) + (−1 − 1 + 3) + m_S = −1 − β_S + m_S.
- **S type X.** S has no bridge. A doubly used bridge at an X point needs an all-multiple
  face S T U with T, U ∈ {P1, Q, P2} consecutive, i.e. {T,U} = {P1,Q} or {Q,P2}. But P1's
  sectors next to P1→Q are its cap triangles P1QX1 and P1QX3, with simple X's; the same
  holds at P2. So β_S = 0, and Φ ≥ −1 − 2 = −3.
- **S with one block.** m_S ≥ 0 and β_S ≤ 3, so Φ ≥ −4.
- **S with no block.** m_S = 3, so Φ ≥ −1.
- **S bent.** Its partner must have 0 or 1 blocks, so it is Q. Then three points are bent
  toward Q, which Claim 1 forbids. Φ ≥ −4 in all cases, so this sub-case is closed. ∎

**Claim 3 (partial): two bent points toward different partners Q1 and Q2.**
- *The bound to beat.* The points are {P1, P2, Q1, Q2}, with Q1 and Q2 having ≤ 1 block.
  If either Q has no block, Φ ≥ −β − 2 + 3.
  - By C19 every bridge triangle avoids P1 and P2, and on 4 vertices that forces a
    triangle-free bridge graph. So β ≤ 4, and Φ ≥ −3 in the no-block case.
  - Otherwise both Q's have one block, Φ ≥ −β − 2, and only β = 4 is dangerous. The bridge
    graph is then the 4-cycle P1Q1, Q1P2, P2Q2, Q2P1.
- *Proven sub-case.* Suppose P1's extra bridge goes to Q2 = V0(P1) or V4(P1), and P2's to
  Q1 = V0(P2) or V4(P2).
  - Then Q2 lies on a cap line of P1, which is a line of Q1. So Q1 and Q2 share a line M,
    and M is a cap line of both P1 and P2.
  - On M, the vertices Q2, X1(P1), Q1 are consecutive, and so are Q1, X1(P2), Q2. Hence
    X1(P1) = X1(P2) =: X.
  - X is simple, so P1's middle line and P2's middle line both equal the second line N
    through X.
  - So N carries P1 and P2, and they are not consecutive on it (X lies between them).
    Therefore σ ≥ β + 1 and Φ ≥ −(β − 1) − 2 = −5. Closed.
- **Open sub-case.** An extra bridge goes along the back ray (R = V5). By the C07 fix this
  needs both V0 and V4 multiple, so they are the other two points.

**k = 4 with β > 0 is NOT yet closed.** Status of the sub-cases:
- (iii) bent points: closed except the V5 sub-case above;
- (ii) bent points with V0 or V4 multiple, outside the 4-cycle: not done;
- (i) the end-vertex exceptions to C20: not done;
- all-multiple faces with one or two X vertices: not done.

**SAT form.**
- For all Q and all pairs P1, P2 with bent(P1→Q) ∧ bent(P2→Q): P1, P2 and Q are collinear,
  and Q is in no block pattern.
- At most 2 bent points per Q.

**Empirical.** There are too few bent points in the samples (13 in the referee's run, 47
bent points with extra bridges in mine) to test pairs. Untested.
