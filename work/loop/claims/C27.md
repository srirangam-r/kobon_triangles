# C27 — sector lemma: in (A), matched singleton ends are within cyclic distance 5 at infinity

**Lemma (proven, general).** Let X be a simple point with two rays ρ ⊂ a and ρ′ ⊂ a′ from X.
Then the number of line ends at infinity strictly inside the arc between the ends of ρ and
ρ′ equals the number of lines crossing ρ ∪ ρ′ exactly once. This is the arc belonging to
the sector (ρ, ρ′), and it has fewer than n positions.
- *Proof.*
  - A line with an end in that arc has its antipodal end outside the arc, because the four
    rays at X split the circle at infinity into the four sector arcs. So the line enters
    the sector and must cross its boundary.
  - It crosses each of a and a′ at most once.
  - A line crossing the boundary exactly once has exactly one end in the arc; a line
    crossing it twice or not at all has none. ∎

**Application to C16 (A).** Take a mutual pair P, Q with X = a_P ∩ a_Q, where both points
are 2nd on their axes (C26 (c)).
- The ray of a_P from X through P to infinity carries only the vertices P and X_1.
- The ray of a_Q from X through Q carries only Q and X′_1.
- So the lines meeting these two rays are:
  - m = PQ, which meets both, so it crosses twice;
  - c_P, the third line at P;
  - C_P, the cap at X_1;
  - c_Q and C_Q, likewise at Q.
- **Hence at most 4 line ends lie strictly between the two singleton ends of a matched
  pair** (on the side of the sector PXQ). Precisely: the ends there are the members of
  {c_P, C_P, c_Q, C_Q} that meet the two rays exactly once. A member meets both rays when,
  for example, c_P = C_Q passes through X′_1.
- If no other singleton lies between them, this count is even (wedges pair cyclic
  neighbours, C17), so it is 0, 2 or 4.

**Kills / prunes.** In each wedge case of work/k6z (fixed S), the perfect matching of C26 (c)
must pair singletons at cyclic distance ≤ 5. With 6 singletons, most of the 15 matchings are
excluded outright. S sets with no admissible matching are UNSAT without search, for example
a singleton isolated by more than 5 positions on both sides.

**Count.** Of the 3,668 admissible u = 6 singleton sets (wedges.py rules, positions 0 and
18 excluded), **only 32** admit a perfect matching with every pair at cyclic distance ≤ 5.
The script is inline in the round-9 log and is easy to redo from `work/t3/wedges.py`. So
(A) drops from about 3,668 cases to 32 before any solving.

**SAT form.**
- For every matched pair of singletons at positions s, s′ with cyclic distance > 5 in both
  directions: forbid the match (a unit clause on the match variable of C26).
- Within distance ≤ 5, the 2 or 4 intermediate ends belong to lines among the four named
  ones. Their lines pass through P, X_1, Q or X′_1:
  - the line owning each intermediate end is c_P, C_P, c_Q or C_Q;
  - that is, it passes through the 2nd or 1st vertex of ℓ or ℓ′ from the singleton end.
- Cheap encoding: for each intermediate end (line L), L crosses ℓ at ℓ's 1st or 2nd vertex
  from e, or crosses ℓ′ at its 1st or 2nd vertex from e′.

**Empirical.** The general lemma is standard and was not separately tested. The application
concerns 94s only.
