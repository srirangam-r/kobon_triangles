# C35 — splitting lemma: at k = 5 (and at k = 6 once k = 5 is closed) every triple point has at least 2 triangles in each alternating sector class

**Lemma (proven, lines or pseudolines).**
- *Setting.* Let P be a triple point with sectors s0..s5, where s_i = (r_i, r_{i+1}). Put
  t_even = #triangles among s0, s2, s4 and t_odd = #triangles among s1, s3, s5.
- *Claim.* Translating one line of P by a small ε, in one of the two directions, gives an
  arrangement with one fewer triple point, no new multiple point or parallel, and
  **T′ = T + 1 − t_even**. The other direction gives **T′ = T + 1 − t_odd**.
- *Proof.*
  - The split creates a new triangular face Δ. The six sectors become the six faces around
    Δ, and they alternate between sharing an edge with Δ and sharing only a vertex.
  - The three edge-neighbours gain one edge each. These are an alternating class, and the
    direction of translation chooses which class. The three vertex-neighbours keep their
    edge count.
  - Nothing else changes for small ε. So T′ = T + 1 − (#triangles in the chosen class).
- *Tested.* 6,714 triple points (gallery plus mutations, n = 12–20): the two split counts
  always equal {T + 1 − t_even, T + 1 − t_odd} exactly (`work/t3/split_test.py`).

**Corollary (proven).**
- Suppose there is no 94 with k − 1 triple points (and no 4-fold point).
- Then in any 94 with k triple points, every triple point has **t_even ≥ 2 and t_odd ≥ 2**.
  Otherwise splitting gives a 94 with k − 1 triple points.
- **k = 5:** this applies now, since k ≤ 4 is closed (C1–C4, C25 refereed).
- **k = 6:** it applies once k = 5 is closed for all β.

**Immediate consequences at k = 5 (proven).**
- Two-block points (X, bent) and centroids automatically satisfy the condition: each has
  2 cap triangles in each class.
- **A 1-block point Q** (block middle r0, triangles in s5 and s0) needs one more triangle
  of each parity. Every option forces a doubly used bridge:
  - s2 and s3: a bridge on r3;
  - s2 and s1: bridges on r1 and r2;
  - s4 and s1: bridges on r5 and r1;
  - s4 and s3: bridges on r5 and r4.
  - Here "bridge" means a doubly used first segment with a triple far end. A block there
    would make b ≥ 2.
  - **So there is no 1-block point without a bridge, and e(Q) ≥ 1, with e(Q) ≥ 2 unless
    the bridge is on r3.**
- **A 0-block point** needs ≥ 4 triangle sectors, 2 of each parity. That forces ≥ 2 doubly
  used rays, all bridges. **So e ≥ 2.**
- **In particular: at k = 5, β = 0 forces every point to have 2 or 3 blocks,** so B = 10
  and all points are type X (no centroid without bridges). This re-derives C14's B = 10
  branch and kills B = 9 without C18.
- **A bent point's partner** (0 or 1 block) has P's two cap triangles next to the ray Q→P,
  one of each parity. It needs one more triangle of each parity.

**Kills / use.** This is not a full kill of β > 0. It removes every configuration with a
low-triangle point: a 1-block point without a bridge, a 0-block point with < 2 bridges, and
in general any point with a sparse alternating class.

**SAT form (cheap and local; valid for the k = 5 instances now, and for k = 6 after k = 5).**
For a triple t = {A,B,C} with A < B < C (slot order before P = the label order), and a
triangle tri(x,y,L) with x, y ∈ t and L ∉ t:
- Its sector at P is given by the directions of L's crossings on x and y relative to P. On
  x, L is before P iff before(x, L, y). Write left/right.
- Sector classes:
  - **even:** (A-left, B-left), (C-left, A-right), (B-right, C-right);
  - **odd:** (B-left, C-left), (A-right, B-right), (C-right, A-left).
- Mixed pairs that are not adjacent rays cannot carry a triangle at P.
- Constraint: z[t] → at least 2 of the 3 even sectors carry some triangle, and at least 2
  of the 3 odd sectors carry some triangle. Each sector is an OR over L of
  tri(x,y,L) ∧ direction literals.
