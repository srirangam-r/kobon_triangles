# C11 verdict: Lemma D (parity through triple points)

**Verdict: refereed-correct.** This is the correct form of the per-line parity rule.
It is the K = 0 case of the corrected C6 rule (see C06.md), with a sharper hypothesis:
(ii) only asks for L's own entries at its triple points, which is all the stretch
argument uses.

## Logic

- **Vertex count.** L has r = n − 1 − j vertices, so there are n − 2 − j entries.
- **No touch at simple crossings implies:**
  - zeros are isolated. A (0,0) pattern at a simple crossing is a touch. A zero next to a
    triple point is excluded by (ii), and so is a triple end vertex.
  - At simple crossings the signs alternate. A repeat would give a doubly used piece
    whose far end is multiple (L1), which is a cap role, excluded by (i).
  - A "both" entry between two simple crossings is impossible (L1). Next to a triple
    point it is excluded by (ii).
  - Every run end is next to a zero at a simple crossing. Its line is one-sided, and
    step 5 forces the common sign σ.
- **Parity.** For each run, length ≡ 1 + q_run. Summing, n − 2 − j ≡ q + 1, i.e. j + q is
  odd. ✓
- **"Applies automatically".** At a type-X point R with axis ≠ L, L's two entries are the
  cap triangles in the sectors S01 and S34. The non-cap sectors S23 and S50 are not
  triangles:
  - a triangle there would make B± doubly used;
  - with a simple far end that would be a third block, impossible;
  - with a multiple far end it would be a bridge, which by Lemma B needs an
    all-multiple face.
  - So the entries are single and opposite, and q = 0. ✓

- **All claim: 0 failures.**
