## Lemma

- **O1a.**
  - A triangle in s1 or s4 would make r1 or r5 doubly used with a simple far end, i.e. a
    block adjacent to r0, which is impossible. This is C20(a)'s step, tested on 3,418
    points.
  - So C35's two extra triangles at k = 5 are s2 and s3. They make r3 doubly used, and its
    far end is triple: otherwise Q would have 2 blocks.
  - Every other ray needs s1 or s4. So Q has exactly 4 triangles and e = 1, on r3. ✓
- **O1b.** The cap crosses r1 or r5 at its first vertex, which is triple. So x_Q ≥ 1, and
  the O1b point's m is at least 1. ✓

## Refined master and the all-one-block kill

- The refined master inequality is the C37 inequality plus #O1b, with the pairs of x
  assigned to their own points. The algebra checks. ✓
- **All-one-block kill:**
  - β ≥ 2β − σ ≥ 6 + #O1b;
  - 2β = Σe ≤ #O1a + 4#O1b;
  - together these give #O1b ≥ 7 > 5, a contradiction. ✓

## Centroid-arm extension

The verdict C08 argument uses only the two blocks next to the ray P→Q and where their caps
meet P's other lines. For a centroid arm Q2 (middles 1 and 3), the relevant crossing is
ℓ1 ∩ C3 = Q4, which lies on the X3 side of Q2, playing the role V4 plays for a bent point.
The other crossings behave in the same way. So:
- every centroid neighbour has at most 1 block, with its middle on the arm line beyond it;
- C21 Claim 1 carries over to any mix of bent and centroid arms: at most 2 arms per target,
  and 2 only if the target is O0.

The script checks centroid and bent loads separately, which only keeps more
configurations.
