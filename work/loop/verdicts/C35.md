# C35 verdict: splitting lemma

**Verdict: refereed-fixed.** The combinatorics and the SAT table are correct. One wording
issue needs fixing.

**The fix.** "Translating one line" of a straight-line arrangement also destroys every other
multiple point on that line. State the split instead as a **local pseudoline modification
at P**: resolve the triple crossing into a small triangle. That is enough, because every
k ≤ 4 result (C1–C4, C25), and the k = 5 closure, holds for pseudolines. Nothing else on
the line changes, no 4-fold point is created, and parallels are irrelevant.

## Checked

- **Lemma.**
  - The new face Δ has 3 edges and 3 vertices. The 6 old sector faces become its 3
    edge-neighbours and 3 vertex-neighbours, alternating around Δ, so one sector parity
    class is the edge-neighbours.
  - Each edge-neighbour gains exactly one side, since P is replaced by two vertices. A
    triangle there becomes a quadrilateral, and a non-triangle stays one.
  - Vertex-neighbours keep their side count. The two resolutions pick the two classes.
  - So T′ = T + 1 − t_class. ✓
- **Corollary.** If t_class ≤ 1, then T′ ≥ T ≥ 94 with k − 1 triple points. ✓
- **k = 5 consequences.**
  - The two options at a 1-block point force bridges on r3, r1&r2, r5&r1 or r5&r4. A simple
    far end there would give an adjacent block, a type-X block or a bent block.
  - A 0-block point needs 4 triangle sectors, one missing sector of each parity. That gives
    at least 2 adjacent pairs, and those are bridges.
  - With β = 0 every point is type X and B = 10. ✓
- **SAT table.** In the wiring, A, B, C run top to bottom before P, so the cyclic ray
  order is B→, C→, ←A, ←B, ←C, A→. The adjacent pairs and their two alternating classes
  match the table exactly.

## Test (`work/referee4/c35_test.py`)

- Data: 2,400 contracted and flipped arrangements, 13,363 triple points.
- Method: classify each triangle at P by the table, using the solver's before(x, L, y).
  Then resolve P both ways ('g g+1 g' and 'g+1 g g+1') and recount T.
- Result: {T′₁, T′₂} = {T + 1 − t_even, T + 1 − t_odd} in every case, and no triangle
  falls in a non-adjacent pair. **0 failures.**
