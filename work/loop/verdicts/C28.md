## Claim 1: non-collinear paths

**Γ is simple.** a_P meets a_{P″} (or a_B, a_{P′}) at neither P nor X_1. The reasons: F1,
C_P is non-triple, and each axis piece carries only its own point. So the listed vertices
are Γ's only vertices.

**The face side R.**
- F3 puts all mutual faces on one side R. The side of Γ along a_A's segment equals the
  side of a_A.
- At each turning point X, the sector of the face is exactly R's corner there. So both
  continuations beyond X lie outside R, and they never re-enter R: each axis meets the
  adjacent axis only at X.

**Counting ends in R's arc.**
- Every other line meets a_P, and must do so at X_1, P, or beyond X (outside R). So no
  line lies wholly inside R.
- The non-Γ lines through Γ's vertices are:
  - the m's, which cross twice with their middle segment in a face in R, so both ends lie
    outside R;
  - C_P, c_P, c_{P″}, C_{P″} (or the P′ versions), which cross once.
  - These four are pairwise distinct: F1 (c's carry one point), F2 (C's distinct and
    non-triple).
- So R's arc holds exactly 4 ends, and the distance is exactly 5. ✓
- The same holds for two degree-2 points (P–A–B–P′), and for a singleton edge via C27 plus
  F1 and F2 (all four lines distinct and each meets exactly one ray). ✓

## Claim 2: collinear sub-cases

**Shape (i), P, A, P″ on m.**
- X and X′ lie on opposite sides of m, because m crosses a_A at A, which lies between
  them. The faces PAX and P″AX′ lie next to Γ′'s straight segment on m, on those
  respective sides.
- The rays a_P→X and a_{P″}→X′ meet Γ′ only at P and P″:
  - a_P ∩ m = P;
  - a_P ∩ a_{P″} avoids P″ and X″_1 (F1, F2).
  - So they lie on opposite sides of Γ′.
- A line through no vertex of Γ′ would meet both rays, and so it would cross Γ′ at a
  point that is not a vertex. That is impossible, because Γ′ consists of edges and
  vertex-free rays.
- So all 18 lines pass through X_1, P, A, P″ or X″_1, and those carry only 9 lines.
  Contradiction. ✓

**Shape (ii), A collinear and B not.**
- Along the segment [P, B] on m, Γ‴'s side containing ABX_AB is opposite to the side
  containing PAX.
- At B, F3 puts B→A and B→P′ on the same side of a_B. So both a_B rays lie in one sector
  of Γ‴ at B, and the faces ABX_AB and BP′X_{BP′} lie on the same side.
- Hence the two rays lie on opposite sides. The vertex lines number 11 < 18.
  Contradiction. ✓

**Shape (ii), both collinear.**
- P, A, B, P′ all lie on m. Each consecutive pair on m is mutual, so m is nobody's axis. A
  triple point Q on m would force Q′ on m as well, so j ∈ {4, 6}.
- m caps nothing: mutual caps are axes, and non-mutual caps are non-triple lines.
- All triple points on m are type X and non-axis, with β = 0.
- So C11 (with the C15 fix) makes m touch an unused segment, which contradicts Z = 0. ✓
