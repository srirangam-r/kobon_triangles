# C55–C59 verdict: no centroid at k = 6; the kN_fixed2 filter

**Verdict: refereed-correct**, including the script check. One caveat applies to C56 and
C58: they use type O1b, whose k = 6 status (C42 needs C35) is noted in C54.

## Claims

**C55.** The pairs (Q_i, Q_j) lie on a triangle side with a simple cevian foot between
them. So each is a consecutive multiple pair that is neither an edge nor a face side,
which gives σ + 3.
- The script adds these pairs into the same union as the C45 and C49 gaps, so there is
  no double count.

**C56.**
- An O1b point's side ray has a triple first vertex T, and T is consecutive with q.
- Arm-givers lie on the ray opposite the middle (C08/C40), so T is never one of them.
- If q's only bridge or face partners are givers, then (q,T) is an edge that is neither a
  bridge nor a face side, and it is distinct from every gap pair (gap pairs are never
  edges).
- This gives ⌈n/2⌉, since two O1b points may be each other's T.

**C57.**
- A partner's rays toward X1 and X3 have simple first vertices.
- The cevian beyond a partner starts at the simple far end of its block middle, unless the
  partner is O0.
- So the consecutive triple neighbours are P, the two side extensions, and the
  cevian-beyond for O0.
- A point R bridged to two partners would lie on two of the lines C1, C5, ℓ0, …, and every
  such intersection is a partner, P or a foot. ✓

**C58.** A centroid arm lies on the partner's block line b_Q, not on m_QR. ✓

**C59.**
- Slack 0 forces all three C50 credits to be exceptions. (i) is impossible for O1a, so
  (ii) holds: each Q_i is 2nd on its cevian.
- So the rays P→Q_i carry only Q_i and Y_i.
- By the sector lemma at P, each arc holds at most 5 ends. For example, arc (r0,r2) holds
  ℓ1's r1-end, C5, C3, C_{Q0} and C_{Q2}, while C1 crosses both rays. Each sector is less
  than π, because it contains no opposite ray.
- That makes 3 + 15 < 36 ends. Contradiction. ✓

## Script (`kN_fixed2.py`)

- K = 5: **32 rows, identical to p11_fixed**.
- K = 6: **1,910 (c35) / 2,084 (noc35)** centroid-free graphs.
- The only centroid survivor is (X,X,C,O1,O1,O1), with bridges only (2,3), (2,4) and
  (2,5) and slack 0, exactly as C59 treats. ✓
- The C54 fixes (O1b-only third-vertex rules) and the joint C49 minimisation are in place.
