# C62 verdict: twin faces cannot have crossed blocks

**Verdict: refereed-correct.** The lemma is right, for lines and pseudolines and at any k. It kills graphs 1, 12, 14, 16,
18 and 19.

## Proof check (re-derived)

- **Step 1.** Δ1 and Δ2 are distinct faces on [a,b], so they lie on opposite sides of it. At a they fill the two sectors
  next to a→b.
  - So a→P1, a→b and a→P2 are consecutive, and they lie on three distinct lines. The circular order at a is as stated. ✓
- **Step 2.** The neighbour of P1→a other than P1→b is ρ, on P1's third line. The block covers P1→−b, ρ and P1→a.
  - The cap triangle P1–X1–a fills the sector at a next to a→P1 other than Δ1, which is (a→−P2, a→P1).
  - So X1 = first(a→−P2), and the cap is m_aP2. ✓
- **Step 3.** The second apex is M1 = first(P1→−b), on m_bP1. It lies on the cap, and on the cap the order is a, X1, M1.
  - So M1 = m_aP2 ∩ m_bP1 lies on m_aP2 beyond a, away from P2. ✓
- **Step 4.** The same holds for P2 with P1 ↔ P2 and a ↔ b. Then M2 = m_aP2 ∩ m_bP1 lies beyond P2, away from a. ✓
- **Step 5.** The lines meet once (m_aP2 ≠ m_bP1, since a, b, P1 are not collinear). The two rays of m_aP2 are disjoint.
  This is a contradiction. ✓
- **Corollary.** By C24 F1, a type-X third vertex covers both of its face rays. So two type-X third vertices give crossed
  blocks. ✓
