# C13 verdict: axis lemma through other triple points (j′ + q even ⇒ the axis touches)

**Verdict: refereed-correct.**

## Logic

- **Setup.** a has r = n − 2 − j′ vertices. At P = X_p, the entries t_{p−1} and t_p are
  "both". Hypothesis (ii) gives t_{p−2} = t_{p+1} = 0, or a ray.
- **Stretches.** There are m_1 = p − 3 and m_2 = r − p − 2 inner entries, so
  m_1 + m_2 = n − 7 − j′.
- **The vertex next to a forced zero is simple.** For example X_{p−2}: if it were a triple
  point, it would have a zero entry, which (iii) excludes. X_{p±1} are simple cap points.
  So the one-sided argument applies at every run end.
- **Other triple points on a lie strictly inside the stretches**, since X_{p±1} are cap
  points and X_{p±2} are simple by the previous step. Inside the stretches:
  - (i) gives rule (d) at simple crossings;
  - (iii) makes the entries next to them single;
  - L1 excludes "both" between simple crossings.
- **Parity.** Each touch-free stretch has m_i ≡ q_i + 1. For p ≥ 3 the sum gives
  n − 7 − j′ ≡ q, i.e. j′ + q odd. For p = 2 or p = r − 1 there is one stretch:
  n − 6 − j′ ≡ q + 1, the same condition. m_i = 0 is the (0,0)-touch case, and is
  consistent. ✓

## Tests (`work/referee4/c13_c15.py 1500 13 18 18-1 16 20 22`; even-n gallery plus 6,000 mutated, 13,445 arrangements)

- 3,091 axes satisfy (i)–(iii) with j′ + q even. **All touch.** Among them, j′ ≥ 1 in 87
  cases: (j′,q) = (1,1): 26; (2,2): 2; (4,0): 59.
- Odd cases: 9 of 55 have no touch, e.g. (3,0) and (5,0). So the parity is the real
  constraint.
