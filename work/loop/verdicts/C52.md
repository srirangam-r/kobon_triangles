## Checked

**Rich points.** A point is rich iff t ≥ 7, or t = 6 with opposite gaps.
- There are 8, 6 and 4 doubly used rays respectively.
- Adjacent middles share their cap: the sector between them is a triangle whose third
  side passes through both simple far ends.
- So runs of middles have length ≤ 2, and the rays separating them are bridges.
- t = 8: D ≤ 5, e ≥ 3. t = 7: D ≤ 4, e ≥ 2. ✓

**m′ = h + x − e/2, with h = 12 − 2D − caps.**
- t = 8: every cap's span ends in bridge rays, so x = #caps and h + x = 12 − 2D. This gives
  m′ ≥ 0.5 at D = 5, 2 at D = 4, and 3.5 at D ≤ 3.
- t = 7: m′ ≥ 1, even taking x = 0.
- Standard point: m′ ≥ 2.
- Summing σ − 2β ≥ −Σe/2 gives Σm′ ≤ −6, so the triple points need
  Σ m′ ≤ −6 − 0.5·#rich. ✓

## Corrections

1. **Not every rich t = 6 point is standard.** Rich t = 6 points with bridges exist: in my
   samples, 62 of 103 had (D, e) = (3,1), (2,2) or (1,3). They have m′ ≥ 4.5, so the
   consequence stands.
2. **D-count.** "Triple points contribute ≤ 0 otherwise" fails for a 1-block point with
   e = 5, which contributes +0.5. That needs k ≥ 6.
