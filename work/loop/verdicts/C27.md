# C27 verdict: sector lemma, and the distance ≤ 5 matching in C16 (A)

**Verdict: refereed-correct.** The general lemma, both ray claims, the "≤ 4 ends, so cyclic
distance ≤ 5" step and the count (3,668 → 32) all check.

## The general lemma

- Let X be simple, with rays ρ ⊂ a and ρ′ ⊂ a′ from X. The four ends of a and a′ split the
  circle at infinity into the arcs of the four regions of a ∪ a′.
- The arc of the sector W between ρ and ρ′ is antipodal to the arc of the opposite region,
  so it contains no antipodal pair.
- A line M ≠ a, a′ (not through X) crosses the boundary ρ ∪ ρ′ of W b ∈ {0,1,2} times, and
  each crossing switches in or out of W.
  - b = 1 ⟺ exactly one end of M lies in W's arc.
  - b = 0 or 2 ⟹ no end in the arc, since both ends inside would be an antipodal pair.
- So #ends strictly inside = #lines crossing ρ ∪ ρ′ exactly once. ✓ This holds for
  pseudolines too.

## The application in (A)

**Where X sits on each axis.** By C26(c), X = a_P ∩ a_Q is the 3rd vertex from the
singleton end on both axes.
- In (A) every point has exactly one mutual side, and its other side is a ray (u = 6).
- The C16 addendum shows each mutual block is in exactly one pair. So P's mutual side and
  Q's mutual side meet at the same X, which is the non-singleton cap point of each.

**The two rays carry only P, X_1 and Q, X′_1.** Hence the ray of a_P from X through P
contains only P and X_1, and its end is P's singleton end. The same holds for Q. ✓

**The lines that can cross the rays.** The only lines crossing these rays are:
- m = PQ, which crosses both, twice in total, and so contributes no end;
- c_P, c_Q (the third lines at P and Q), and C_P, C_Q (the caps at X_1, X′_1).

a_Q meets a_P only at X, the apex. Within {c_P, C_P, c_Q, C_Q}:
- c_P ≠ c_Q, because P and Q share only m;
- c_P ≠ C_P, because a cap avoids its point;
- any coincidence, such as c_P = C_Q or C_P = C_Q, crosses both rays and drops out.

So **≤ 4 ends lie strictly inside the sector arc, and the cyclic distance on that side is
≤ 5.** The other side has ≥ 31 positions, so the sector arc is the short side. ✓

**Parity.** The arc's boundary ends are singletons, so wedge ends inside it pair among
themselves (C17). With no singleton inside, the count is 0, 2 or 4. ✓ This is already
implied by the admissibility runs.

## SAT form

- Forbidding matches at cyclic distance > 5 is sound.
- The cheap intermediate-end condition is a necessary condition, so it is sound. It says
  the owner line passes through the 1st or 2nd vertex of ℓ from e, or of ℓ′ from e′. It is
  also true of m, which harmlessly weakens it.

## Tests

- **Sector lemma:** 330,792 sectors (4 per simple vertex) in 600 pseudoline arrangements
  (gallery, contractions, flips), with ends placed at positions L and L + n. **0 failures**
  (`work/referee4/c27_sector.py`). This also confirms the end-position convention used by
  wedges.py.
- **Count:** `work/referee4/c27_count.py` reuses wedges.py's admissible(), excludes positions
  0 and 18, and requires a perfect matching with all pairs at cyclic distance ≤ 5. It finds
  **3,668 admissible sets → 32**, matching the claim and the coordinator's recount.
