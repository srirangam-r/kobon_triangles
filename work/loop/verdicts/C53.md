# C53 verdict: 4-fold points as bent or centroid-arm partners

**Verdict: refereed-fixed.** (a) and (c) are correct. In (b), "P stays bent toward Q" does
not always follow.

## (a) The partner rule fails for 4-fold partners

- The ray order at Q is forced: Q→P is flanked by C1→X1 and C2→X3, and opposite rays are 4
  apart. So L4 sits at positions 2 and 6.
- A block at C1→X1 then needs its cap ℓ1 to pass through the first vertex of L4a, not of
  C2′. The triple-point obstruction therefore disappears. ✓

## (b) The non-rich 4-fold partner

- Moving L4 (the local move, per the C51 fix) turns Q into a triple point with T′ ≥ T. ✓
- **But P need not stay bent toward Q.**
  - With j = 2 for L4, the two gaining sets are G = {s1, s3, s4, s6} and
    G′ = {s0, s2, s5, s7}.
  - P's two cap triangles at Q are s0 and s7. Both lie in G′.
  - If the only gaining set with at most 2 triangles is G′, then t(G′) = 2 exactly
    (s0 and s7), which can happen at t(Q) = 5. The move then destroys P's cap triangles,
    and P is no longer bent.
- **Fix.** Say "yields a 94 with one fewer 4-fold point" (valid). Keep "P stays bent" only
  when t(G) ≤ 2.
