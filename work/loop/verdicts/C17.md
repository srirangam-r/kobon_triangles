# C17 verdict: mutual ends are slope-adjacent (wedges), and Statement 2 (Z = 0)

**Verdict: refereed-correct**, for Statement 1, Statement 2 and the SAT form. **The SAT
form is sound for kobon_sat's labelling and `before` semantics and can go into k6z.**

## Statement 1, logic

- **The wedge is empty.** X is simple and an end vertex of both L and R. W is the region
  between their rays beyond X; its boundary has no vertex other than X.
- **No line ends inside W's arc.** Suppose a (pseudo)line M ≠ L, R has an end strictly
  inside W's arc at infinity.
  - The other end of M is antipodal, so it lies in the arc of the opposite region W′.
    (The four ends of L and R cut the circle into the arcs of the four regions of
    L ∪ R, and the antipodal map sends W's arc to W′'s.)
  - So M must leave W, which means crossing a ray beyond X or passing through X.
  - Either would give a vertex beyond X, or make X non-simple. Contradiction.
- **Hence the two ends are adjacent** in the circular order 0L … (n−1)L, 0R … (n−1)R.
  - The adjacent pairs are (iL, (i+1)L), (iR, (i+1)R), ((n−1)L, 0R) and ((n−1)R, 0L).
  - This gives the stated cases:
    - first/first ⇒ |L − R| = 1;
    - last/last ⇒ |L − R| = 1;
    - mixed ⇒ {0, n−1}, in either orientation.
- The statement is symmetric under reversing the sweep direction, so it does not depend
  on which end is called "first". ✓

## SAT form, checked against `search/kobon_sat.py`

- **Semantics of `before`.** `before(r,i,j)` is `ng[sorted]` if i < j, else `pz[sorted]`,
  with lines labelled by slope.
  - I computed χ from 10,000 pseudoline arrangements, using χ(i,j,k) = +1 iff the crossing
    of i and k is above j, and 0 if concurrent.
  - `before` then equals the **left-to-right order on every line, in every arrangement**:
    0 exceptions.
  - The labels are the left order, top to bottom. So the model's labelling is exactly the
    wiring order that Statement 1 needs.
- **The clauses.**
  - first(L,R) := ∧_{M ≠ L,R} (¬before(L,M,R) ∧ ¬z[L,R,M]). This correctly says "L∩R is
    L's first vertex and is simple". The ¬z part is needed: a concurrent M makes both
    `before` literals false.
  - Each forbidden combination is one clause with no auxiliary variables. For example,
    ¬(first(L,R) ∧ first(R,L)) = ∨_M (before(L,M,R) ∨ before(R,M,L) ∨ z[L,R,M]).
  - About 4 clauses per pair, so ≤ 612 clauses at n = 18, each of ≤ 3(n−2) literals.
- **Case split (complete).**
  - |L − R| ≥ 2 and {L,R} ≠ {0, n−1}: forbid all four combinations.
  - |L − R| = 1: forbid the two mixed combinations.
  - {0, n−1}: forbid first/first and last/last.
- **Symmetry breaking** (rotation relabelling, or "line 0 …"): each admissible labelling is
  the slope order of a rotated copy, so the clauses remain valid.

## Statement 2 (Z = 0, no triple point at a line end), logic

- Let X_1 be simple with t_1 = s single.
- R_1's piece on side −s borders only the face over L's ray and the face over l_1 on side
  −s. Neither is a triangle, so the piece is unused.
- Z = 0 then makes it a ray, so X_1 is an end vertex of R_1.
- The triangle over l_1 on side s has its side on R_1's bounded piece at X_1, so the
  corner face is a triangle. ✓
- k6z clause: first(L,R) ∧ "t_1 single" ⇒ first(R,L) ∨ last(R,L). It is valid only under
  Z = 0, which k6z assumes, and it uses the simplicity already contained in first(L,R). ✓
