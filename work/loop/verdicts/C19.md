# C19 verdict: bent point P → Q with a further bridge [P,R]

**Verdict: refereed-fixed.**
- (a), (b), the kill of the C08 "−6" configuration, and the SAT clause are correct.
- Two consequences stated in C19 are wrong in general:
  - "the bridge graph has no triangle through a bent point" is false for k ≥ 5 (true at
    k = 4, see the fix);
  - the "cheaper" encoding "β(k=4) ≤ 2 whenever a bent point exists" is not implied.

## Logic

**(a)** Rays 1 and 3 are block middles with simple far ends, and ray 2 ends at Q. So R is
the first vertex of ray 0, 4 or 5. ✓

**(b)** Q's lines are PQ, C1 and C2 (verdict C08).
- R = V0: V0, X1, Q are consecutive on C1, because both cap-triangle sides are edges on
  opposite sides of line(1,4). Also R ∉ PQ (PQ ∩ line(0,3) = P) and R ∉ C2 (C2 ∩ C1 = Q).
- R = V4: the same argument with C2 and X3.
- R = V5: P lies between Q and R on PQ, and C1 and C2 meet PQ only at Q.
- In every case Q and R are not consecutive. ✓

**Kill.** In the C08 configuration the bridge [Q,R] cannot exist. ✓

**SAT clause bent(P→Q) ∧ dblbridge(P,R) ⇒ ¬cons(Q,R): sound.**
- One might worry that the encoded "bent" does not force X1 and X3 to be simple. It does
  in every real or pseudoline arrangement. tri(a,b,d) and tri(b,c,d) are faces sharing
  [P,X1], and together they fill the half-plane on P's side of d near X1. A third line
  through X1 would split one of them.
- The encoded "bent" also matches centroid points, which also have blocks 1 and 3. (b)
  holds for them too: their extra bridges go to V0 and V4, and ray 5 is a middle.

## What is wrong

**1. "The bridge graph has no triangle through a bent point" is false in general.** (b)
only excludes the edge QR. A triangle P, V0, V5 is possible.
- The C07 7-line exact example (`work/referee4/c7_bent_example.py`) has bridges [P,V0],
  [P,V5] and [V0,V5], a bridge-graph triangle at k = 5.
- Contracted arrangements gave 14 more such triangles, all with k ≥ 5
  (`work/referee4/c19_test.py`).

**Fix, true at k = 4.**
- Bridges to both V0 and V4 force sectors (4,5) and (5,0) to be triangles. So ray 5 is
  doubly used. Its far end V5 is either simple (then P is a centroid, not bent) or a fifth
  triple point.
- So at k = 4 a bent point has bridges only to Q and at most one of V0, V4, i.e. e ≤ 2.
- A triangle through P would therefore need the edge QR, which (b) forbids.
- Every downstream use (C21, C23, C24) is at k = 4, so they are unaffected.

**2. "β(k=4) ≤ 2 whenever a bent point exists" does not follow.** C19 excludes only QR.
A 4-cycle P–Q–W–V0 is not excluded, for example with W on C2 beyond Q and a bridge
[W,V0] on V0's third line. **Do not encode this.** The correct k = 4 facts are e(bent) ≤ 2
and no bridge triangle through a bent point.

## Tests

- 10,000 contracted arrangements (n = 10–18), 52 bent points, 68 extra bridges (35 to V0,
  25 to V4, 8 to V5): (a) and (b) had **0 failures**.
- There were 14 bridge-graph triangles through bent points, all with k ≥ 5, plus the
  exact 7-line example.
