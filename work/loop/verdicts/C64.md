# C64 verdict: a bent partner is never a face vertex with both face sides doubly used

**Verdict: refereed-correct.** The lemma holds for lines and pseudolines, at any k. It kills graphs 26 and 27, and
independently 14 and 16.

## Proof check (re-derived)

- **Inputs.**
  - V's cap triangles V–X1–Q and V–X3–Q fill Q's two sectors next to Q→V, and X1, X3 are simple. This is the bent
    structure itself; no C35 is needed.
  - Q's block middle, if any, is opposite Q→V. This is C08, re-derived k-independently in the C34 verdict (c).
- **Setup.** [Q,P] and [Q,R] are doubly used, so (q5,q0) and (q1,q2) are triangles. V ∉ {P,R}: a flanking triangle
  would have the triple vertex R or P.
- **j = 2 and j = 5.** The flanking triangle (q1,q2) or (q5,q0) is V's cap triangle, and its third vertex is R or P,
  which is triple. ✗ ✓
- **j = 3.** first(q2) is the simple X of V's cap triangle (q2,q3). q2 is flanked by the triangles (q1,q2) and (q2,q3),
  so it is doubly used with a simple far end, i.e. a block with middle q2 ≠ q0 = opposite(q3). ✗ ✓
- **j = 4.** Symmetric (the middle would be q5 ≠ q1). ✓
