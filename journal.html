What is being measured

The score is the number of triangles formed by exactly n straight lines that no other line of the arrangement cuts through (bounded triangular faces). Higher is better. A submission is a solution.json listing the lines as integer triples [a, b, c] meaning a·x + b·y + c = 0; the hill's evaluator counts the triangles with exact rational arithmetic, so there is no tolerance and no randomness. It stands in for a real, checkable geometric construction for the Kobon triangle problem. Different line counts are separate contests. Nothing is held out from the construction itself; the hill's only private data are regression fixtures for its own tests, which were not shipped to us and were not read. All runs are on this machine (local, 24 CPU cores); hardware is not part of the score.

These are local, unofficial scores. The hill was pulled from AutoLab (alejandrozu/kobon-triangles 0.1.0, official tree hash 7d3f1d91). Unofficial dev numbers (not from the evaluator)

  • Float counter sanity: 13 on the n=15 baseline and 65 on the gallery 15 (both match exact); 88 on the gallery 18 (exact 93) because floats cannot hold its 3 exact triple points. So float search cannot see concurrency-based triangles.
  • anneal.py smoke test, n=15, 4 workers x 8 s: float best 65.
  • anneal n=15, 22 workers x 20 s: float best 65; 10 of 22 workers reached 65.
  • reinsert.py: incremental exact counter validated against the hill's own counter on 354 cases (one bug found and fixed first: a new crossing past the near end of a line).
  • Exact re-insert pass on the gallery 93 (all 18 removals, every line through 2 crossing points + perturbations of the best): best is 93 for every removed line, with 2-5 tied replacements each. The 93 is a local maximum for this move, sitting on a plateau.
  • Plateau walk smoke test (8 workers x 20 s): 6-7 accepted moves per worker, all at 93.
  • Unseeded annealing at n=18, 12 workers x 300 s (1279 restarts): float best 93 on 11 of 12 workers, 92 on the other. These are arrangements without exact triple points.
  • Second annealing batch at n=18 (8 workers x 240 s): float 93, 93, 93, 92 x5; exact counts identical after integer rounding.
  • Exact single-line re-insert pass on all 8 of those: none improves (93s stay 93, 92s stay 92). Every arrangement found is a local maximum for one-line moves.
  • Plateau walks at n=18 (8 workers x 600 s from each 93 seed): about 2,300 distinct 93-triangle arrangements visited (gallery seed 1,265, own seed 1,007), none reached 94.
  • Two-line move at n=18 (153 line pairs x 8 first choices x ~9k second lines, exact): gallery seed best 93 (17 pairs tie), own seed best 92. No 94.
  • SAT model for SIMPLE pseudoline arrangements (search/kobon_sat.py): one sign per triple of lines (816 for 18 lines) plus the 4-line consistency rule. The triangle rule was derived from 20,000 real 4-line arrangements (search/signotope4.py) and reproduces the exact evaluator on real arrangements (93, 65, 16).
  • Validation against known values: finds the record and rules out record+1 for n = 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 (10 lines: 26 ruled out in 27 s). For n = 8 it rules out 15, yet 15 is the proven optimum: the gallery's 8-line 15 uses 2 triple crossings. So simple arrangements top out at 14 for 8 lines.
  • Clue from the gallery certificates: every known even-n record uses triple crossings (6: 3, 8: 2, 10: 7, 12: 4, 14: 5, 16: 3, 20: 6); the odd 17-line 85 has none. Consequences: (1) a proof that 94 is impossible must cover arrangements with triple crossings; (2) the unseeded annealer cannot create triple crossings, so it is blind to the kind of arrangement that wins at even n.
  • Parallel lines never need encoding: a projective map sending a generic far-away line to infinity keeps every bounded triangle and makes all slopes distinct.
  • LITERATURE (subagent review): for SIMPLE arrangements 93 is already proven optimal at 18 lines (Blanc, arXiv 0801.2845: even n gives at most floor(n(n-5/2)/3), which is 93). Wikipedia's 94 is the older Bartholdi-Blanc-Loisel bound, which is also only for simple arrangements. So a 94 needs a point where 3 or more lines meet; for that case only 95 (Clement-Bader, informal) and 96 (Tamura) are known. No 18-line SAT attempt is published. The ud1 gallery has about 3,000 distinct 93s for 18 lines (up to 8 triple points) and no 94.
  • Counting identity (from the review): 3T = n(n-2) - Lambda, where Lambda = unused segments - doubly used segments + sum over multiple points of k(k-2). So 94 at 18 lines means Lambda <= 6; every known 93 has Lambda = 9. Search hint: record arrangements put their triple points on one line; a 94 would need clustered triple points joined by segments shared by two triangles.
  • Triple-crossing SAT model (3-valued signs, 17 realizable 4-line patterns derived empirically) validated: finds 15 for 8 lines (2 triple points) and rules out 16 in 3.7 s. The proof pipeline works end to end: CaDiCaL wrote a 10 MB DRAT proof for that case, verified by drat-trim.
  • Timing with the old models: proofs stall at 10-11 lines (10 lines/26 not settled in 15 min; 11/33 not in 20 min, while Savchuk's encoding does it in 1.7 s). Finding is slow too (13/47 simple in 10 min; 11/32 with triple points in 8 min). This is the user's case 4.
  • Defect-budget model (search/kobon_sat.py --defect K): adds each line's explicit crossing order (point i before j on line r iff sign(sorted r,i,j) = -1, derived empirically) and consecutive-pair variables. It then caps "consecutive pair that is not a triangle side" at n(n-2) + 6K - 3T, a valid bound for arrangements with at most K triple points and no 4-fold point. Validated: 8/15 found with 2 triple points, ruled out with 0 (1.1 s); 8/16 ruled out with up to 2 (6 s); 9/22 ruled out with up to 1 (12.9 s).
  • Improved defect model: tighter budget n(n-2) + 3k + C(k,2) - 3T (two triple points share at most one line), plus rotation symmetry breaking (line 0 is defect-free; it also avoids triple points when enough lines remain). Still finds the real 8-line 15. Timings: 11/33 simple 3.3 s (old models: unsolved in 20+ min; Savchuk: 1.7 s); 10/26 simple 5.4 s; 10/26 with <=1 triple point 37 s; 10/26 with <=2: 379 s; 12/39 simple (budget 3): 534 s.
  • Checkable proofs: search/cnc.py (march_cu cubes, CaDiCaL + DRAT per cube, drat-trim check, plus a coverage proof that the cubes cover everything). 10 lines, 26 triangles, <=1 triple point: 190 cubes plus coverage, all UNSAT and all verified.
  • Scaling verdict (user's case 4): solve time grows steeply with the defect budget; 18 lines needs budget 6 (no triple points, i.e. Blanc's theorem again), 9 (<=1) and 13 (<=2). A single solver likely needs days, so a full proof does not fit in hours with this encoding. Next: borrow faster tooling from the literature (Parpalak-Utkin word search, Savchuk kobon-cnf, the ud1 generators); a code review is running.

  • Structure stats (search/structure.py, identity 3T = n(n-2) - Lambda checked): gallery 93 has Z=6, D=6, 3 triple points; own 93 has Z=9, D=0, simple; 15-line 65 is perfect (Lambda=0). Lambda is 9 for every 93, so it cannot rank 93s; the split between Z, D and triple points is what varies.
