## 1. General lemmas

**Lemma 1 (four consecutive rays). PROVED.** Let v be a vertex on k lines, with 2k rays in cyclic order r_0, …,
r_{2k−1}, and let ℓ be a line not through v. Then ℓ meets exactly k cyclically consecutive rays r_i, …, r_{i+k−1}, in
this order along ℓ, and between two consecutive meetings ℓ stays in the open sector between those rays.

*Proof.* The k lines through v cut the plane into 2k sectors. ℓ meets each of the k lines once, and never at v. Each
meeting moves ℓ from a sector to an adjacent one. A ray belongs to one line, so ℓ never meets a ray twice; hence the
walk through the sectors is monotone, and its rays are k consecutive ones. They are on distinct lines because r_j and
r_{j+k} are the two rays of one line. ∎

**Lemma 2 (sealing). PROVED.** Let B be a vertex on lines ℓ1, ℓ2. Let ρ1 ⊂ ℓ1 and ρ2 ⊂ ℓ2 be rays from B, with first
vertices U1, U2, and let Θ be the closed wedge between them. Suppose ρ1 has no vertex beyond U1 and ρ2 none beyond
U2. Then every line meeting the interior of Θ passes through B, U1 or U2.

*Proof.* Let ℓ meet the interior of Θ and miss B, U1, U2. ℓ meets ℓ1 and ℓ2 once each. If it met ∂Θ = ρ1 ∪ ρ2, the
meeting would be a vertex of ρ1 or ρ2 other than B; the only ones are U1 and U2. So ℓ misses ∂Θ and lies inside Θ. But
ℓ meets ℓ1 somewhere, and the part of ℓ1 inside Θ is ρ1. Contradiction. ∎

*Use.* At B+ take ρ1 = B+→(−α+) and ρ2 = B+→(−γ−), so U1 = U− and U2 = U+. Call this wedge **Θ**. It contains the
ray B+→(−b). The parts of Θ in S_{2,3} and S_{1,2} are Θ_L and Θ_R. Lemma 1 at P shows directly that:
- γ− meets rays 2, 3, 4, 5 at B+, C−, T−, Y−, so B+→(−γ−) lies in S_{1,2};
- α+ meets rays 2, 1, 0, 7 at B+, A+, T+, Z+, so B+→(−α+) lies in S_{2,3}.

**Lemma 3 (TO frontier). PROVED.** A triple has at most one unbounded ray. If it has one, then every sector not
adjacent to that ray is a triangle. A quad of our class (all-8) has no unbounded ray.

*Proof.* The two sectors beside an unbounded ray contain it in their boundary, so they are not triangles. Two
adjacent sectors lie in different alternating classes. So each class {s_i, s_{i+2}, s_{i+4}} has a zero, and TO
(sum ≥ 2) forces its other two members to be 1. Two unbounded rays at a triple would either be adjacent (three
consecutive zero sectors) or two apart (four consecutive zeros). Either way some class has two zeros, which TO
forbids. (Opposite rays belong to one line, which then has no other vertex; that is impossible since n > 2.) An all-8
quad has all its sectors triangular. ∎

**Lemma 4 (H has a non-bad end; W = 14). PROVED, uses U = 0.** In C3, C4 and C6, at least one end of H is non-bad.

*Proof.* T± are full and V± are triples or simple (W14_stars 5.1). H's weight Σ(m−1) from V− to V+ is 13, 12 or 11
when 0, 1 or 2 of V± are simple; the total is 17. Look at the + end.
- **V+ simple.** V+ is a two-fan: its triangles T+Z+V+ and T+V+Y+ both lie beside V+T+, and V+Y+, V+Z+ are single
  (W14_stars 5.1). The continuation V+→(−H) is unused. U = 0 makes it unbounded, so H ends at V+ with an I-end.
- **V+ a four-run triple.** V+→(−H) is unused. η0 = 0 makes it unbounded: an F-end at V+.
- **Five-run.** Excluded by Wbig_ends 4.3. Say V+SW1+ is a face and (−e2, −H) is not. Then V+S is single.
  - W1+ = c∩e1 (face V+Z+W1+, Z+ a triple) is multiple: a simple W1+ would put S on c, so S = P (wrong ray).
  - So S is simple (else M\*) and SW1+ is double (else O\*). If W1+ is a triple, the second face on SW1+ has apex
    c∩H = P (wrong ray). If W1+ is a quad, SW1+ is a kite block and V+S would be double.
  - The mirror case uses W2+ = a∩e2.
- **V+ full**, with S the next H-vertex. If S is simple, Wbig_ends 4.4 gives SW1+, SW2+ single, so S is a two-fan.
  U = 0 then makes H end at S with an I-end (weight 1 beyond V+). If S is multiple, the weight beyond V+ is ≥ 2.

So a bad + end needs V+ full, S multiple and a further vertex: weight ≥ 3 beyond V+. Two bad ends need total weight
≥ 6 beyond V±. But a bad end needs V full (a triple), so both V± would be triples and the weight beyond them is only
17 − 13 = 4. ∎

**Lemma 5 (removed slope edges). PROVED from NOTE3 §5 and Wbig_ends 1.2.** Order the lines cyclically by slope. G_W
(join two lines when their crossing is a bad wedge) is the slope 18-cycle C18 minus exactly π = 18 − W edges, the
**removed edges**. A line has as many non-bad ends as removed edges at it. Two consequences:
- (a) If two lines have consecutive ends at infinity, they are slope-adjacent. If moreover they cross at a multiple
  point, their slope edge is removed: a bad-wedge vertex is simple.
- (b) If a line has a non-bad end, some removed edge contains it.

The cyclic order of the 2n ends at infinity is (ℓ1, …, ℓn, ℓ1, …, ℓn), so consecutive ends at infinity belong to
cyclically slope-adjacent lines, and an unbounded face with two unbounded edges on L, L′ makes L, L′ consecutive. ✓

**Slope arcs.** By Lemma 1 at P, a line not through P that meets rays i, …, i+3 has its ends in S_{i−1,i} and
S_{i+3,i+4}. Every skeleton line other than H, a, b, c meets ray 2 or ray 6 together with three neighbouring rays. So
its slope lies strictly between a and b (the **AB arc**: φ+, γ−, e2, e4, D−, and e1 when it meets b−) or strictly
between b and c (the **BC arc**: D+, α+, α−, e3, and e1 when it meets b+). In particular **H's two slope neighbours are
a, c or free lines**, and H∩a, H∩c = P is multiple.

## 2. C4 (Y3s+ and Z3s−, on the diagonal)

Facts (W14_stars §5.3, x-image of Y3s+): U− = D−∩α+ is simple, B+U− is double, G = b∩D+∩D−. Ring at G:
GU+ (D+), GB+ (b), GU− (D−), G→(−D+), G→(−b), G→(−D−). The faces B+U+G and B+U−G fill (GU+, GB+) and (GB+, GU−).

**Step 1.** U+ is a two-fan. Its sectors (A+,B+) and (B+,G) are triangles. (G,−γ−) is not, since GU+ is single, and
(−γ−,A+) = Π_A is not. So B+U+ is a block whose continuation U+→(−γ−) is unused. If this is bounded, U+ is a touch
centre (U ≥ 1). Likewise for U−→(−α+).

**Step 2 (sealing).** Suppose neither return carries a touch. Then B+→(−γ−) has no vertex beyond U+, and B+→(−α+) none
beyond U−. By Lemma 2 every line inside Θ passes through B+, U+ or U−: it is b, D+ or D−. The vertices of b beyond B+
lie inside Θ, so the only one is G. **So G→(−b) is unbounded.**

**Step 3.** At the triple G the sectors (GU+, −D−) and (−D+, GU−) are not triangles (the single edges GU±). By Step 2

Lemma 3: G's unbounded ray −b forces triangles in the sectors (GU+,−D−) and (−D+,GU−).)

**Theorem C4. PROVED.** C4 forces a touch at U+ or at U−, so U ≥ 1. **At W = 14 (U = 0), C4 is impossible.** For any
W, C4 needs U ≥ 1, hence π ≤ 3 and W ≥ 15. Inputs: W14_stars §§5.1–5.3, Lemma 4.1, TO, η0 = 0 (none used beyond

## 4. C6, the two cases at U+

### 4.1 Case a: GU+ single

**Step 1.** U+ has exactly the two triangles A+U+B+ and B+U+G, so B+U+ is a block with unused continuation
U+→(−γ−). If this is bounded it is a touch (U ≥ 1). Assume **no touch at U+**. Then γ− has no vertex beyond U+, and by
Lemma 3.1 α+ has none beyond U−.

**Step 2 (the cap region).** By Lemma 2, the lines meeting Θ are b, D+ (through U+), D− and e4 (through U−). Their
crossings inside Θ:
- D+∩D− = K0, D+∩e4 = G, b∩D− = R (Lemma 3.2);
- D+∩b = G and D−∩e4 = U− lie on the boundary of Θ.

Hence:
- D+ has no vertex beyond K0;
- e4 has none beyond G (in Θ_R the only lines are e4 and D− beyond R, and they meet at U−);
- b and D− have none beyond R;
- R is simple, so R is a **bad wedge** for b and D−: its opposite face GK0R is a triangle.

The unbounded faces of Θ, in order, are:
- Ξ = [α+ ray, U−K0, D+ ray];
- [D+ ray, K0R, b ray];
- the wedge at R;
- [D− ray, RG, e4 ray from G], which is G's sector (−b, −e4);
- [e4 ray, GU+, γ− ray], which is G's sector (−e4, GU+).

So the top ends at infinity read **α+, D+, b, D−, e4, γ−**, consecutively.

**Step 3 (removed edges).** By Lemma 5(a), {α+, D+} (A+), {D+, b} (G) and {D−, e4} (U−) are removed. γ− ends at U+
with a double last segment B+U+ (an I-end, non-bad). So by Lemma 5(b) a removed edge contains γ−. It is not one of the
three, which gives **π ≥ 4**. Then π + U = 4 forces π = 4 and U = 0, so W = 14. Lemma 4 now gives a removed edge at H.
H is not among {α+, D+, b, D−, e4, γ−}, and γ−'s other neighbour lies in the AB arc or is a; neither is H. So **π ≥ 5**.
✗

**Theorem C6a. PROVED.** In C6, if GU+ is single, U+ carries a touch. In particular this case is impossible at
W = 14. It is also impossible at every W unless U ≥ 1 is spent at U+.
