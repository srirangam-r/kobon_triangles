- They use the forced structure of W14_stars.md §§5–6.

- The intact corners force a rigid chain of triple points (Lemma I).
- The chain runs into the crossing budget of 17 per line.

### 0.2 Conventions
These are (P1)–(P2) of W13_caseE §0.
- **Rings.** At a triple whose rays A, B, C (on three distinct lines) are consecutive, the ring is
  A, B, C, −A, −B, −C. The neighbours of A are −C and B. The neighbours of C are B and −A.
- **Two adjacent faces fix the ring.** Two triangular faces at a triple v that share a ray give three consecutive
  rays, and so the whole ring.
- **The face across an edge.** Let uw be an edge bordering a face F. The face across uw (from F) lies, at u, in the
  sector between u→w and the other neighbour of u→w (the neighbour not used by F). Likewise at w.
- **Triangles.** If the face across uw is a triangle uwt, then t is the first vertex on that ray at u and also on
  the corresponding ray at w (P1).
- **Single and double.** An edge is *double* if both of its faces are triangles, and *single* if exactly one is.
- **Simple vertices.** At a simple vertex the ring is ℓ, m, −ℓ, −m.

### 0.3 Lemma T: the payment lemma (PROVED)
**Lemma T.** In C2 and C5, the bounded N-tokens that no payment rule selects are exactly the four endpoint tokens
of the two failing edges. Hence:
- **(T0)** Every edge of an all-multiple triangular face is double, except the two failing edges.
- **(T1)** Let uv be a single edge with u and v both multiple, and not a failing edge. Then the face on uv is uvx
  with x simple and a kite centre.
- **(T2)** Let uv be a single bounded edge with u multiple and v simple, and let uvx be the face on it. Then
  either:
  - (a) x is simple and ux is double; or
  - (b) x is multiple and both xv and xu are double.

*Proof.*
1. **Δ_N = 4.**
   - Here Δ = 4, by H = 2 with ΣS° = K3 = K4 = 0.
   - By ALL8_NOTE3 (6), Δ = Δ_Z + Δ_N with Δ_Z = 2Z − U − G_Z ≥ 0.
   - Δ_N = N_res − F is the number of bounded N-tokens (tokens on single or unused first segments at multiple
     points) that the injective payment scheme does not select. This follows from ALL8_NOTE2 §4, ALL8_NOTE3 (1)–(4)
     and §3, and the case-A K1 payment.
   - The four M\* tokens of the two failing edges are unselected (ALL8_NOTE5 (4)).
   - So Δ_N ≥ 4. Hence Δ_N = 4, and these four are the only unselected bounded tokens.
2. **The selection rules.** These are all of them.
   - (K0) Both endpoint tokens of two single caps of a triple-only kite.
   - (K1A) Both endpoint tokens of the single cap of a case-A K1 kite.
   - (O) The outer-triangle rule. Take a block PX (P multiple, X a simple 2-fan or 3-fan) and a face PXT on it
     with TX single. Select the token at P on PT if PT is single, and the token at T on TX if PT is double.
     - This covers H_3, U_3, I_3, and the U_4/I_4 reservations, which use the same rule.
   - In K0 and K1A, the face on a selected cap is the kite triangle, and its third vertex is the simple kite
     centre.
3. **(T1).**
   - The token (u, uv) can be selected by a K-rule only if x is a kite centre.
   - The O-rule with PT = uv selects only the token of P = u (say). This needs a block ux, with uvx an outer face
     of x, so that xv is single.
   - The other token (v, vu) would need a second block vx, with uvx an outer face for it. That would make xu
     single, a contradiction.
   - The O-rule with TX = uv needs X ∈ {u, v} to be simple, which is false.
4. **(T2).**
   - A K-rule needs both endpoints to be multiple, which fails.
   - The O-rule with P = u, T = v and X = x gives (a).
   - The O-rule with T = u, X = v and P = x needs PT = xu double, and xv is then the block. This gives (b).
5. **(T0)** is (T1) with x multiple. ∎

**Remark.** Lemma T is stronger than W14_stars (E). For instance, it also forbids the unselected token at T in an
outer face of a 3-fan when PT is single and T is multiple.

### 0.4 Lemma NQ: P is the only quadruple point (PROVED)
1. Let Q ≠ P be a quadruple point.
   - By ΣS° = 0 and W14_stars §0, Q is an isolated DB star.
   - By Theorem 1.4 and Lemma 1.5 applied to Q, Q has a single edge in Σ_a(Q), and that edge carries an unselected
     token.
2. Every Σ_a(Q) edge lies on one of the faces T_QA_QY_Q, Q A_Q B_Q or A_QY_QU_Q. Each of these contains Q or a kite
   corner A_Q of Q.
3. **What a kite corner needs.** A kite corner of an all-8 point has a double edge to that point and a double block
   to the simple kite centre.
4. By Lemma T, the unselected token lies on a failing edge of P. The face on that edge is A+Y+U+, or in C2 the face
   F+Z+U+′, or in C5 the face C−Z−U−. So one of these faces contains Q or a kite corner of Q.
5. **All their vertices are triples.** Check A+Y+U+ (the other faces are its y- or x-images):
   - **A+.** Its only quadruple neighbour is P.
   - **Y+.** Its rays Y+A+, Y+T+ and Y+V+ go to triples. Y+U+ is single, and Y+→(−φ+) borders the non-triangle
     Ψ′, so it is at most single. Only Y+W2+ is left, so Y+ cannot have both a double edge to a quadruple point
     and a double block.
   - **U+.** Its rays U+B+, U+A+ and U+G go to triples. U+Y+ is single, and U+→(−γ−) borders Ψ′. Only U+K2 is
     left, so the same argument applies.
6. Hence there is no such Q. ∎

So every multiple point other than P is a **triple**. This is used without comment from now on.

### 0.5 Lemma E: end types (PROVED)
Work at the + end. The − end is its image.
- **(E1)** V+ is **four-run** or **full**. Four-run means the sectors (−e2, −H) and (−H, −e1) are not faces.
  - *Four-run.* The ray V+→(−H) is unused. Since η0 = 0 (Lemma T), H ends at V+.
  - *Five-run is impossible.* Suppose the face V+SW1+ exists and the sector (−e2, −H) is not a face, so V+S is
    single.
    - W1+ is multiple: otherwise the face V+SW1+ puts S on c, so S = c∩H = P, on the wrong ray.
    - If S is multiple, (T1) fails, since W1+ is not a kite centre.
    - If S is simple, (T2) needs W1+S double. The face across W1+S then has its third vertex on c beyond W1+ (in
      W1+'s sector (k(S), −c)) and on H beyond S. That vertex is c∩H = P, on the wrong ray.
- **(E2)** At a **full** V+, let S+ be the next vertex on H. The faces V+S+W1+ and V+W2+S+ exist.
  - W1+ and W2+ are multiple, by the same wrong-ray argument.
  - Write k1 for W1+'s third line (through S+) and k2 for W2+'s third line. Then W1+ = c∩e1∩k1 and
    W2+ = a∩e2∩k2.
  - The rings are W1+: c(Z+), e1(V+), k1(S+), −c, −e1, −k1 and W2+: a(Y+), e2(V+), k2(S+), −a, −e2, −k2.
  - The faces V+Z+W1+ and V+Y+W2+ are all-multiple, so Z+W1+ and Y+W2+ are double (T0).
- **(E3)** Suppose S+ is **simple** (k1 = k2 =: k). Then S+W1+ and S+W2+ are single.
  - The face across S+W1+ would lie at S+ in the sector (S+W1+, S+→(−H)), and at W1+ in the sector
    (k(S+), −c).
  - Its third vertex would be c∩H = P, on the wrong ray.
- **(E4)** Suppose S+ is a **triple** (k1 ≠ k2). Its ring is k2(W2+), H(V+), k1(W1+), −k2, −H, −k1.
  - V+S+W1+ is all-multiple, so S+W1+ is double.
  - The face across S+W1+ is S+W1+J1+, where J1+ is the first vertex on S+→(−k2) and also the first vertex on
    W1+→(−c). So J1+ ∈ c∩k2.
  - Likewise S+J2+W2+ is a face, with J2+ ∈ a∩k1.

### 0.6 Lemma CL: crossing counts (trivial)
Every line meets each of the other 17 lines exactly once.

Call a line **complete** when its listed vertices already account for all 17 other lines. Then:
- the line has no further vertices;
- it ends at its two extreme listed vertices;
- each listed vertex carries exactly the lines listed there.

## 1. Two corner lemmas

### 1.1 Lemma B: a broken c-corner (PROVED)
**Lemma B.** Suppose Z+U+′ is single (the corner (+,c) carries UZ+), so Z+'s sector (−α+, −e2) is a non-triangle
Ψ. Then:
- **(B4) V+ four-run.**
  - W1+ is simple and Z+W1+ is double.
  - The first vertex Q1 on Z+→(−α+) is **Q1 = α+∩e1**. It is simple, and it is also the first vertex on e1
    beyond W1+.
- **(BF) V+ full.**
  - Q1 = α+∩k1 is simple, and W1+Q1 is double.
  - The next vertex on α+ after Q1 is X = α+∩e1, which is also the first vertex on e1 beyond W1+.
- **In both cases, e1 meets α+ beyond Z+**, on the side away from T+.

The images:
- **(+,a), y-image:** if Y+U+ is single, e2 meets φ+ beyond Y+. In case (BF) the vertices are Q1′ = φ+∩k2 and
  X′ = φ+∩e2.
- **(−,c), z-image:** if Z−U− is single, e3 meets α− beyond Z−.

*Proof.*
- **(B4).**
  1. V+W1+ is single, because V+'s sector (−H, −e1) is empty.
  2. Hence W1+ is simple: a multiple W1+ would violate (T0) on V+Z+W1+.
  3. If Z+W1+ were single, (T2) for Z+'s token would need x = V+ with V+W1+ double, which is false. So Z+W1+ is
     double.
  4. The face across Z+W1+ lies at Z+ in (−c, −α+). At the simple W1+ (ring W1Z, W1V, W1(−c), W1(−e1)) it lies in
     (W1(−e1), W1Z). So Q1 ∈ e1.
  5. Z+Q1 is single, because Ψ lies across it. If Q1 were multiple, (T1) would make W1+ a kite centre. But W1+'s
     sector (W1V, −c) is not a face. So Q1 is simple.
- **(BF).**
  1. Z+W1+ is double (E2). The face across it, s_Z = Z+W1+Q1, lies at W1+ in (−k1, c(Z+)). So Q1 ∈ k1.
  2. Z+Q1 is single, and Q1 is simple by (T1).
  3. (T2) with x = W1+, which is multiple, forces W1+Q1 to be double.
  4. The face across W1+Q1 lies at W1+ in (−e1, −k1), and at the simple Q1 along α+ beyond Q1. So its third vertex
     is X = α+∩e1. ∎

### 1.2 Lemma I: an intact c-corner at a full end (PROVED)

- **V− is full;**
- **(h)** the first vertex on α+ beyond M′ is not on e1. In C2 and C5 this follows from Lemma B, because e1 meets

Then:
- **(I1)** W1− = c∩e3∩k3 and M′ = α+∩α−∩k3 are triples, where k3 = W1−S−.

- **(I2)** R1, the first vertex on M′→(−α−), is **R1 = α−∩e1∩ℓ**, a triple, with face M′R1L2.

- **(I4)** W1−Xz is double. The face across it is W1−J1−Xz, where J1− is the first vertex on c beyond W1−.
  **J1− ∈ ℓ.**

*Proof.*
- **(I0) V− is not four-run.**

  3. M′ is a triple with consecutive rays α+(U−), α−(Z−), e3(W1−). So its ring is α+(U−), α−(Z−), e3(W1−),

  5. The face across U−M′ lies at U− in (−α+, −D−), so its third vertex is L2. At M′ it lies in (−e3, α+(U−)).

  6. But e3∩D− = U−′ ≠ L2. Contradiction. With five-run excluded by (E1), V− is full.

  - **Suppose R1 is simple.**

## 2. C2 is impossible (PROVED)
